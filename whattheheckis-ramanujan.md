## PART I: THE BIOGRAPHY THAT MATTERS (brief — the credential)

**Srinivasa Ramanujan (1887–1920), Erode, Tamil Nadu.**

- Self-taught: no formal mathematical training; worked alone on a
  slate since age ~16, recording results in notebooks
- Failed his college exams twice (math only — everything else
  failed); became a clerk at the Madras Port Trust
- 1913: the letter to G.H. Hardy (Cambridge) — nine pages of
  formulas, no derivations
- 1914–1919: Cambridge with Hardy; Trinity Fellow (1918), FRS
  (1918) — among the youngest ever
- 1920: died at 32 — the "lost notebook" (the last year's work)
  found in 1976 at Trinity, containing the mock theta functions
- The frame: a devout Brahmin Vaishnavite whose entire working
  life was solitary calculation

**The credential matters:** unlike Russell (39 days of
illumination after a life in art), Ramanujan's "revelation" came
after **20 years of saturated practice** — he had been doing
mathematics daily since adolescence. The register was soaked in
the structure before the dreams delivered it.

## PART II: THE REVELATION CLAIMS (his own account)

**The Namagiri account (from his own statements and the
biography, Kanigel's The Man Who Knew Infinity):**

> "An equation for me has no meaning, unless it expresses a
> thought of God." — Ramanujan

> "While asleep, I had an unusual experience. There was a red
> screen formed by flowing blood. As I was gazing at it, a hand
> began to write on it. I looked at it intently, and I saw the
> formulas of the goddess Namagiri." — (his account of a
> revelation in England)

The goddess Namagiri (of Namakkal — his family's deity) appeared
in dreams; formulas unfolded on scrolls; "drops of blood" were
the ink; the goddess wrote on his tongue. The results arrived
whole, with total conviction, and he recorded them as received.

**The key feature — the delivery was always SUDDEN and WHOLE:**
not derivations, not steps — finished results, entire theorems,
delivered complete. Hardy's later analysis: Ramanujan had no
"working through" — the results arrived, he recorded them, he
moved on.

## PART III: THE VALIDATION (the science — what was actually right)

**The honest core: most of the results were REAL — and some opened
entire fields.**

| Result | Validation | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Partition numbers (p(n)) — the Hardy–Ramanujan formula | The **circle method** — now a major technique in analytic number theory | ✅ validated, foundational |
| The Ramanujan tau function congruences | Proved by **Deligne** via the Weil conjectures (étale cohomology) — a Fields Medal line | ✅ validated, deep |
| Modular equations, complex multiplication | Correct — Hardy: "mastery... beyond that of any mathematician in the world" | ✅ validated |
| The functional equation of the zeta function | He found it himself, unaware of Riemann — correct | ✅ validated (rediscovery + original) |
| Ramanujan primes, Ramanujan graphs | **Expander graphs** — now used in computer science (error-correcting codes, derandomization) | ✅ validated, applied |
| The mock theta functions (the lost notebook, 1920) | **Uninterpretable for 80 years**; Zwegers (2002) found their completion — harmonic Maass forms; now central to **umbral moonshine** and string theory | ✅ validated — a century later |
| The 1/π series | Ramanujan-type series — used in modern π computation | ✅ validated, used |
| 1729 (the taxicab number) | The smallest number expressible as a sum of two cubes in two ways | ✅ (the famous exchange) |
| Some results | Wrong or rediscoveries — a minority, normal for volume | ⚠️ honest note |

**Hardy on the 1913 letter (the quote that matters):**

> "I wrote to him saying that they must be true, because, if they
> were not true, no one would have the imagination to invent
> them."

**Hardy on the limits:**

> "The limitations of his knowledge were as startling as its
> profundity... and yet he had never heard of a doubly periodic
> function or of Cauchy's theorem, and had at best but the
> vaguest idea of what a function of a complex variable was."
