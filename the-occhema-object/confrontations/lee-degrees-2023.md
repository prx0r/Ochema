# Confrontation: Degrees of Consciousness

## Andrew Y. Lee — Noûs 57(3): 553–575 (2023)
### The graded-bearer claim, defended rigorously

**Date:** 2026-07-31 · **Added in:** v1.3.0 · **Result: ALIGNS**

---

## The Source Claims (exact)

> "To be more conscious is to have more of whatever consciousness is.
> Since we don't know what consciousness is yet, we don't know yet
> whether some entities are more conscious than other entities. But
> I'll also argue that most theories of consciousness entail that
> consciousness comes in degrees. Given this, I argue that
> consciousness probably comes in degrees."

The paper: (1) refutes the main objections to degrees of consciousness,
(2) examines the semantics of degreed 'conscious' expressions, (3)
develops an analysis of what it is for a degreed property to count as
degrees of consciousness, (4) applies the analysis to theories.

---

## The Confrontation

### What ALIGNS with the thesis

1. **The graded bearer.** The thesis's L5 (cognitive light cone —
   "biological individuality is graded and can scale"; aperture regimes;
   densities as compatibility levels) claims consciousness comes in
   degrees. Lee argues most theories entail degrees — including, by
   the thesis's own logic, the thesis. **The thesis's graded-bearer
   claim is philosophically defensible per Lee's analysis** (the
   objections to degrees are "unsustainable"). (B/C.)

2. **The "consciousness meter" framing = the bearer-recovery
   methodology.** Lee's opening (a consciousness meter: metal-detector
   beep vs. food-thermometer scale) is the thesis's Q4 (implementation)
   and the bearer-recovery methodology: what does the meter measure?
   The thesis answers: the convergence of the eight layers. Lee's
   answer: whatever consciousness is — which we don't yet know. **The
   thesis and Lee agree on the epistemic status: the identity is open,
   the gradedness is defensible.** (B.)

3. **The application-to-theories move = the confrontation method.**
   Lee applies his analysis to theories of consciousness and checks
   which entail degrees. This is the same operation as the
   confrontation registry: run each theory against a structural claim.
   (C.)

### What CHALLENGES the thesis

1. **"We don't know what consciousness is yet"** — Lee's agnosticism
   is stronger than the thesis's graded identity claim. The thesis
   postulates D_i ≡_int A_i (E, now mainstream via IIT); Lee would
   say the gradedness claim is robust but the identity claim is
   premature. **This is a legitimate epistemic challenge: the thesis
   carries the identity postulate; Lee declines it.** (C.)

2. **The semantics argument** — Lee's analysis of degreed 'conscious'
   expressions (the semantics of comparatives like "more conscious")
   is more sophisticated than the thesis's treatment of "levels" /
   "densities." If the thesis uses "higher density" / "more
   integrated" in a way that presupposes a scalar, Lee's analysis is
   the correct formal treatment of that scalar. **The thesis should
   adopt Lee's degreed-property analysis for its density claims.** (C.)

### What the thesis CHANGES in response

The density/aperture claims (L8, Part 9.1) should be restated using
Lee's analysis: "a density is a degree of [whatever consciousness is],
formalized as the compatibility condition of the register." The
gradedness is not metaphorical — per Lee, most theories entail it,
and the thesis's own bearer-recovery entails it.
