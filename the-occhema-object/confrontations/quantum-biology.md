## The Confrontation

### The central result: quantum coherence SURVIVES warmth — in narrow cases

**Engel et al. (2007):** the FMO complex (a protein scaffold, ~physiological temperature) shows coherent, wave-like transport of electronic excitation energy over the timescales of its transfer — hundreds of femtoseconds. **This is the direct empirical answer to "can quantum effects survive in a warm, wet, noisy biological system?": yes, in narrow, specific, structure-protected cases.**

**The thesis connection — the water-shield defense gets its precedent:**

| Thesis claim | Quantum biology |
|---|---|
| The water shield (QED coherent domains) could protect coherence | FMO shows a biological protein scaffold DOES protect coherence at physiological temperature |
| The orchestration law needs the register | The FMO complex's structure IS the protection — the register's material organization determines coherence survival |
| Material enactment (substrate matters constitutively) | **The FMO complex is the proof: the specific protein scaffold, not abstract physics, enables the coherence** |

**This is the strongest support the thesis's L5 water-shield claim has ever had:** quantum coherence in biology is real, it survives warmth, and it does so BECAUSE of specific material organization — exactly the thesis's claim that the bearer's material regime is constitutive.

**But the honest limit (Wiseman & Eisert + Shatokhin):**
1. The FMO coherence lasts ~10⁻¹³–10⁻¹² s — NOT the 25 msec Orch-OR needs. **The photosynthesis result does NOT rescue the quantum layer of the orchestration.** It proves coherence-can-survive-warmth, not coherence-can-survive-brain-timescales.
2. Shatokhin et al.: whether the coherence is genuine (vs. environment-assisted noise) is debated — the Engel interpretation is not uncontested.
3. The Wiseman–Eisert definition is the discipline: most "quantum biology" is trivial (coherent excitations, tunneling, van der Waals — real but not requiring Hilbert-space training). The non-trivial claims (quantum brain computing, quantum consciousness) are, per the skeptics, implausible.

### The skeptics' concession — the one that matters to the thesis

**Wiseman & Eisert's final section (Quantum free will):**

> "There is a relation between life and quantum physics that may motivate a philosopher, if not a biologist, to try to understand advanced quantum physics. **This is the fact that quantum physics implies an in-principle distinction between (classical) digital computers and human brains: the behavior of the former is predictable, while that of the latter is not.**"

**This is the formal core of the thesis's Material Enactment / CIMC-defeat, conceded by the skeptics themselves:**

| Thesis claim | Wiseman–Eisert concession |
|---|---|
| Finite automata lack modular structure → no moment-time (CIMC defeat) | "An in-principle distinction between classical digital computers and human brains: the former is predictable, the latter is not" |
| Material enactment (substrate matters) | The in-principle unpredictability is a quantum-physical fact about brains, not an engineering limit |
| Doyle's adequate determinism + the orchestration law | Unpredictability (not randomness) is the brain's actual difference from computers |

**The strongest formulation:** the thesis's thermal-time argument (finite automata → trivial modular flow), Kleiner's no-go theorem (verified processors suppress deviations), and Wiseman–Eisert's concession (brains are in-principle unpredictable, computers are not) are **THREE independent statements of the same conclusion**: the brain's substrate differs from computation's substrate in principle, not in degree.

### The quantum-free-will section (relevant to the thesis's L4)

Wiseman–Eisert argue the in-principle unpredictability supports a quantum account of free will (along Doyle-like two-stage lines: quantum indeterminacy provides alternative possibilities; adequate determinism selects). **This aligns with the thesis's L4 (Doyle's two-stage) and the orchestration law — with the same caveat: unpredictability ≠ freedom; the thesis needs the generate→select structure, not just quantum randomness.**

## The Synthesis

**The decoherence debate's real shape (per this confrontation):**

1. **Quantum coherence CAN survive warmth** — the FMO experiment proves it. The "brain is too warm and wet" objection is too broad: specific material organization can protect coherence.
2. **But not at brain-computation timescales** — the narrow cases are femtoseconds-to-picoseconds, not 25 msec. Orch-OR's quantum layer remains refuted-as-stated.
3. **The thesis's water shield gets a precedent, not a proof** — the FMO scaffold shows the mechanism is plausible (specific material organization protects coherence), but no cytosol-scaffold analog has been demonstrated for 25 msec coherence.
4. **The skeptics concede the one claim that matters** — the in-principle brain/computational distinction — which independently supports the thesis's Material Enactment and CIMC defeat.
