# Confrontation: The Structure of Consciousness (Unity Thesis)

## Lowell Keith Friesen — UMass Amherst PhD Dissertation (2013)
### Advisor: Joseph Levine, Louise Antony, Hilary Kornblith

**Date:** 2026-07-31 · **Added in:** v1.3.0 · **Result: ALIGNS (unity) / CHALLENGES (ubiquity of self-consciousness)**

---

## The Source Claims (from the abstract)

> "I defend the thesis that, necessarily, for any subject (of conscious
> mental states) at any time, all of that subject's conscious mental
> states (at that time) are part of a single, maximal state of
> consciousness. I call this thesis the 'Unity Thesis.'"

> "A maximal state of consciousness is a sum of conscious states that
> are i) simultaneous, ii) have the same subject, and iii) all have a
> conjoint phenomenology."

> "I argue that conscious experience is not necessarily accompanied by
> self-consciousness" (the Ubiquity Thesis is false) — against Husserl
> (via Zahavi) and Kriegel.

---

## The Confrontation

### What ALIGNS with the thesis

1. **The Unity Thesis = the unity invariant (Axiom/Invariant: the
   joint state).** The thesis's unity claim (L1: the irreducible
   representation; L7: IIT's integration) is defended rigorously here
   against the hardest counterexample in the literature: split-brain
   data. Friesen defends a maximal-state unity against partial-unity
   and two-streams interpretations. **This is the thesis's unity
   invariant receiving its most rigorous philosophical defense.** (C.)

2. **The Consensus View's three conditions map onto the bearer.**
   (i) simultaneity, (ii) same subject, (iii) conjoint phenomenology —
   these are the joint-state conditions of the bearer: the state ω at
   a time, the subject index ι_i, and the co-presence of contents in
   one field. **The Consensus View is the informal statement of the
   operator-algebraic joint state.** (C.)

3. **Collective/spread consciousness** — Friesen's discussion of
   whether a collective subject can share experiences (the Consensus
   View "does not take off the table") is the thesis's transindividual
   subjects question (ochemamath §10.6: the combination problem). He
   argues these "stretch the bounds of coherence" — a verdict on the
   thesis's open combination problem. (C.)

### What CHALLENGES the thesis

1. **The Ubiquity Thesis rejection.** Friesen argues self-consciousness
   does NOT necessarily accompany consciousness — against Husserl's
   self-givenness and Kriegel's intransitive self-consciousness. **The
   thesis's L6 invariant ("the perceiving subjectivity forever remains
   true to its own nature") and the subject-index claim (ι_i) sit
   close to the Ubiquity Thesis side of this debate.** If Friesen is
   right that minimal self-consciousness is not ubiquitous, the thesis
   must distinguish: the subject index ι_i (which the thesis claims is
   invariant) vs. self-consciousness (which may not be ubiquitous).
   **The thesis's ι_i needs to be disambiguated from minimal
   self-consciousness — this is a genuine refinement.** (C.)

2. **The Mismatch Objection (vs. Rosenthal)** — Friesen's argument
   that higher-order representation cannot account for conscious unity
   without generating the mismatch objection is relevant to the
   thesis's CIMC confrontation: CIMC's second-order perception is a
   higher-order theory, and Friesen's argument (via Rosenthal) is a
   further pressure on it. **The CIMC confrontation gains a
   co-conspirator.** (C.)

### What the thesis CHANGES in response

The subject index ι_i must be formally distinguished from
self-consciousness. The thesis's claim is that ι_i (the modular
fixed-point subalgebra) is the invariant structure — NOT that
self-consciousness is ubiquitous. Friesen's rejection of the Ubiquity
Thesis is compatible with the thesis once this distinction is drawn:
the invariant need not be self-aware.

## Verdict

| Claim | Verdict | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Unity Thesis (maximal-state unity) | **ALIGNS — the unity invariant's rigorous defense** | C |
| Consensus View conditions | ALIGNS — the joint-state bearer | C |
| Ubiquity Thesis is false | **CHALLENGES — requires disambiguating ι_i from self-consciousness** | C |
| Collective/spread consciousness | ALIGNS — verdict on the combination problem | C |
| Mismatch objection vs HOT | ALIGNS — pressure on CIMC | C |

**The net effect: Friesen gives the thesis's unity invariant its
strongest philosophical defense (against split-brain data) and forces
a needed refinement: the subject index ι_i is the invariant, not
necessarily self-consciousness. The thesis's L6 invariant claim
survives the refinement.**
