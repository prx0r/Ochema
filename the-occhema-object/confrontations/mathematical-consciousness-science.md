## The Source

Kleiner's dissertation (LMU, defended May 2025, 23,000 lines extracted) is
the central document of Mathematical Consciousness Science — the field
that formalizes theories of consciousness (IIT, AI-PP/active inference)
and finds REAL holes in them. Its contributions:

1. **Axiomatic definitions of IIT and AI-PP structures** (category-theoretic)
2. **Rigorous derivation of Active Inference** via free energy minimization; proof of compositionality of free energy
3. **New formal problems in IIT's derivation** (not previously noticed)
4. **The unfolding argument, formalized**: if inference and prediction are independent, any minimally informative theory of consciousness can always be falsified — the exact formal underpinning of the "unfolding argument"
5. **The closure paradigm identified**: taking a neuroscientific model as input without allowing consciousness to modify it
6. **A definition of "mathematical structure of conscious experience"** (the structural concept, with Kleiner & Ludwig 2024)
7. **Two no-go theorems on AI consciousness**:
   - If consciousness is dynamically relevant, contemporary AI (CPUs/GPUs/TPUs) cannot be conscious — processors are verified to suppress deviations
   - If computational functionalism holds, consciousness cannot be Turing computation — it must be Hinton's *mortal computation*

## The Confrontation

### What VALIDATES the thesis

**1. The no-go theorem = the CIMC defeat, upgraded from thermal-time argument to direct theorem.**

The thesis's CIMC confrontation (cimc-confrontation.md) argued: finite
automata have trivial modular structure → no thermal time → no
moment-time. Kleiner proves the same conclusion directly:

> If consciousness is dynamically relevant, consciousness must be able
> to "make some difference in its substrate." But the "exact adherence
> of a PU to a pre-set formal system ensures this can't happen" — a
> processor is "designed and verified to behave exactly as specified by
> a formal system," its dynamics "systematically preclude or suppress
> deviations."

**The thesis's thermal-time argument and Kleiner's no-go theorem are
two independent proofs of the same conclusion: substrate-exact
computation cannot host dynamically-relevant consciousness.** The
Material Enactment Principle now has TWO theorem-shaped defenses.

**Grade: A (Kleiner's theorem — proof-grade); the thesis's thermal-time
version is A (math) / C (premise). They converge.**

**2. The "mathematical structure of conscious experience" definition =
the thesis's bridge-hypothesis methodology, formalized.**

Kleiner & Ludwig's structural concept: a mathematical structure is of
conscious experience iff there is a *structural aspect* of experience
that corresponds to it, identified via *variations* — variations
change aspects of experience, and variations may or may not preserve
the structure.

**The thesis connection:** the bridge hypotheses (unity, boundary,
temporality, quality, valence, agency, subject index) ARE proposed
mathematical structures of conscious experience. Kleiner's definition
gives the thesis the *criterion* for what counts as a legitimate
bridge: a structure is of conscious experience iff variations of
experience track it. **The thesis's bridge hypotheses were asserted;
Kleiner gives the test for whether they're well-formed.**

**Grade: A (the definition — published, Kleiner & Ludwig 2024);
C (application to the thesis's specific bridges).**

**3. The three problems Kleiner finds in the naive criterion (MDC) are
the thesis's known weaknesses, formalized:**
- Problem 1 (incompatible structures): the same experience can be
  given incompatible structures (discrete vs non-discrete topology) —
  the thesis's underdetermination claim (physical data underdetermine
  the bearer), now shown to apply to phenomenal data too.
- Problem 2/3 (per the paper): the naive criterion can't distinguish
  legitimate from arbitrary structures.

**The thesis's selector problem (Λₚ) is Kleiner's structural problem,
from the phenomenal side.** The thesis says physical descriptions
underdetermine the bearer; Kleiner shows phenomenal descriptions
underdetermine the structure. **Both sides underdetermine — the joint
fixed point is the response to both.**

**4. The unfolding argument, formalized = the thesis's falsification
conditions, sharpened.**

> If inference and prediction are independent, any minimally
> informative theory of consciousness can always be falsified.

**The thesis connection:** this is the formal statement of why the
thesis's falsification conditions matter and why report-based
validation is fragile. The thesis's own falsification conditions
(Part 7) are partially protected because they're structural (the
triad, exclusion, invariant) not report-based.

**5. Dynamical relevance = the thesis's anti-epiphenomenalism,
formally required.**

> "Consciousness needs to be dynamically relevant... it must be
> relevant to the dynamical evolution of a neuroscientific system over
> and above the dynamical evolution prescribed by the reference
> model... consciousness needs to be understood as a full-fledged
> physical part or process of the brain; it cannot be subjugated to an
> epiphenomenon."

**This is the REPM anti-epiphenomenalist argument (Mørch), given a
formal requirement:** a theory of consciousness that isn't dynamically
relevant cannot even be tested (two theories can't disagree about
reports). The thesis's token-identity (D_i ≡_int A_i) satisfies this;
epiphenomenalism fails it — formally.

### What CHALLENGES the thesis

**1. The structural definition's demand:** Kleiner requires the
correspondence to be *identified via variations*. The thesis's bridge
hypotheses are mostly asserted at C/D without the variation-analysis
Kleiner demands. **The thesis's bridges need the variation-test applied
— this is a genuine methodological upgrade, not decoration.**

**2. The unfolding argument threatens report-based validation**
(which some of the thesis's L8 corpus uses). The thesis's Tier-4
corpus (channeled/regression reports) is exactly the kind of
report-based inference Kleiner shows can be unfalsifiable. **The
thesis's grading discipline (corpus = C/E, never evidence) is the
correct response — but the formal result should be cited as the
reason.**

**3. IIT's formal problems (newly identified by Kleiner)** — the
thesis's IIT 4.0 validation (the identity claim, exclusion axiom)
relies on IIT's structure. If Kleiner's identified problems in IIT's
derivation hold, the validation's foundation shifts (though the
exclusion axiom and identity claim may survive — they're
phenomenological axioms, not derivations).

of experience must pass the variation-test.**
