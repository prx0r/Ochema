## The Confrontation

### The HTVH (peer-reviewed — the important one)

**Claim:** the distribution of valence magnitudes is heavy-tailed (or
approximately logarithmic). Conventional 0–10 scales systematically
underestimate extremes: cluster headaches (extreme pain) and
psychedelic bliss (extreme pleasure) both show magnitudes far beyond
what linear scales capture.

**The thesis connection — the valence bridge hypothesis (ν_i),
empirically anchored:**

| Thesis claim (ν_i) | HTVH |
|---|---|
| Valence = coherence of the constraint structure | Valence has a nonlinear magnitude structure |
| The harmonic ladder (Invariant 6) | Heavy-tailed/logarithmic scaling = the ladder's metric |
| Rasa = geometry = valence (the quality spectrum) | Extreme states are geometrically far from the symmetric center |
| The Tantrāloka's five blisses (innate → consciousness-bliss) | A nonlinear gradient of felt intensity |

**The key convergence (already in our corpus, rasaqri.md §162):**
> "QRI's finding that valence scales may be logarithmic or heavy-tailed
> resonates with the Tantrāloka's five blisses gradient (innate →
> stillness → supreme → great → consciousness-bliss). Both describe a
> nonlinear relationship between the 'depth' of a state and its felt
> intensity."

**The thesis's spectral-quality conjecture** (quality = spectrum of
the modular Hamiltonian) predicts a specific valence structure; the
HTVH provides the empirical shape (heavy-tailed). **This is the first
empirical constraint on the spectral-quality conjecture.** If the
modular spectrum is heavy-tailed (which large-spin ladders are), the
valence scaling follows.

**Grade: B (the HTVH is peer-reviewed, empirically grounded);
C/D (the connection to the thesis's spectral conjecture — still
conjectural).**
