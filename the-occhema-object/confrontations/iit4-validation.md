## The Source Claims (exact quotes)

> "Consciousness can thus be considered as intrinsic, absolute, or
> genuine existence — the only existence worth being — because without
> it there would be nothing at all. Imagine a universe without
> consciousness. In what sense would it genuinely exist, if it would
> not exist for anyone?"

> "IIT's explanatory identity claims that an entity's cause-effect
> structure accounts for all properties of an experience — essential
> and accidental — **with no additional ingredients**... quality is
> structure."

> "To exist intrinsically or absolutely, an entity must have
> cause-effect power upon itself, in a specific, unitary, definite and
> structured manner."

**The five axioms of phenomenal existence:** intrinsicality
(experience exists for itself), information (it is specific),
integration (it is irreducible), exclusion (it is definite),
composition (it is structured).

## The Confrontation

### What VALIDATES the thesis

| OchemaMath | IIT 4.0 | Relation |
|---|---|---|
| D_i ≡_int A_i (identity, not dualism) | "Cause-effect structure accounts for all properties... no additional ingredients" | **The same claim, mainstreamed** |
| The state ω (the register) | The complex in its current state | Same object |
| Exclusion E_i (determination through exclusion) | Axiom 4: "every experience is definite — it is this whole... neither less nor more" | **The same axiom, phenomenologically grounded** |
| Q1 (why anything is manifest) | "Experience is proof that something exists" | IIT answers; OchemaMath leaves primitive |
| Subject pole as modular-invariant subalgebra | Complex = maximally irreducible set (max Φ) | Homologous — both select a distinguished substructure by an extremum |

### What DIVERGES (the testable difference)

**Selection criterion.** IIT selects the subject by Φ-maximization
(the maximally irreducible complex). OchemaMath selects by Takesaki's
modular invariance (the subject pole must be invariant under the
state's own flow). The Tegmark review proved these are *different
invariants*: Φ = cut-mutual-information; the modular flow = the
state's own temporal structure. High Φ does not imply non-trivial
modular structure, and vice versa.

**The consequence:** two selection criteria, one testable divergence.
A system that maximizes Φ but has trivial modular structure (a
highly-correlated static state) would be conscious per IIT, not per
OchemaMath. A system with rich modular structure but low Φ (a
history-bearing, time-structured process with weak current
correlations) would be conscious per OchemaMath, not per IIT.
**Design the experiment; one dies.**

### What the thesis CHANGES in response to IIT

1. The identity claim D_i ≡_int A_i is no longer fringe — it is the
   current leading theory's core. The thesis's grade for the identity
   can be upgraded from "E (fringe postulate)" to "B/C (mainstream
   postulate, empirically validated in the first four postulates)."
2. The exclusion axiom is now independently derived three ways:
   IIT (phenomenology), Takesaki (operator algebras), the collapse
   (physics). One axiom, three derivations.
3. The epistemic posture stays: IIT asserts the identity as its
   founding postulate; OchemaMath holds it as a graded hypothesis.
   Same claim, different discipline — the thesis's grading is its
   differentiator.
