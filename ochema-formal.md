## Abstract

We present a formal framework for recovering candidate physical bearers of conscious processes in biological systems. The framework does not explain why phenomenality exists. It provides a methodology for identifying, ranking, and falsifying candidate bearer models using joint physical-phenomenal convergence.

The framework's central claim is that subject and world are jointly partitioned by a single operation — an exclusion of an undifferentiated possibility domain into a subject pole and a world pole. This exclusion operation is formally defined and physically implemented by the bioelectric organization of living organisms. The framework is substrate-sensitive: the bearer is neither abstract information nor material inventory but the organized activity of a specific embodied medium.

All claims are graded: A (proven), B (experimentally confirmed), C (supported by converging evidence), D (hypothesis), E (speculative). The framework makes eight falsifiable predictions and specifies nine conditions under which it would be seriously weakened.

**Keywords:** consciousness, subject individuation, bearer recovery, dual-aspect monism, bioelectricity, morphogenesis

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The Explanatory Target

A complete theory of consciousness must answer four distinct questions:

| Question | Label | Addressed? |
|---|---|---|
| Why is anything phenomenally manifest? | Existence (Q1) | No — left primitive |
| What individuates one subject from another? | Individuation (Q2) | Partially — bearer recovery methodology |
| What structures the phenomenal field? | Structure (Q3) | Partially — bridge hypotheses |
| Which physical organization implements it? | Implementation (Q4) | Yes — empirically investigable |

The framework does not claim that answering Q4 automatically answers Q1.

### 1.2 The Selector Problem (Λₚ)

Given a conscious organism, which physical process within it is the bearer of that consciousness? Physical descriptions admit multiple partitions at multiple scales — neural, bioelectric, metabolic, mechanical, sensorimotor. Different analytical choices produce different candidate bearers.

**Definition 1 (Selector Problem):** For a conscious organism O with phenomenal field D, identify the physical process A ⊆ O such that D is the intrinsic aspect of A.

The framework does not claim to prove which candidate A is the bearer. It provides a methodology for identifying the best candidate model given available evidence, and specifies how rival models could be discriminated.

**Status: C (supported — the problem is real; no current metric uniquely selects).**

## 2. Formal Framework

### 2.1 Core Identity Postulate

Let D_i denote one phenomenal subject-history and A_i denote one concrete materially enacted process.

**Postulate 1 (Token Identity):**

```
D_i ≡_int A_i
```

where ≡_int denotes numerical identity under epistemically asymmetric access.

The same token is:
- **Extrinsically** describable through causal, biological, material, and behavioral relations
- **Intrinsically** present as one phenomenal trajectory

**Status: E (metaphysical postulate — not derivable within the framework).**

### 2.2 The Epistemic Gap

Third-person propositions do not entail first-person presentation:

```
E ⊬ P
```

But non-derivability does not entail ontological distinctness:

```
(E ⊬ P) ⇏ (E ≠ P)
```

The identity claim is a metaphysical postulate constrained by empirical structural correspondences.

### 2.3 Information Is Not a Mental Substance

**Definition 2 (Information):** Information is a measure of structured difference relative to a specified probability distribution and state description. Shannon information H(X) = −Σ p(x) log p(x).

The framework adopts:

```
Information is a property of organized physical distinctions.
```

not:

```
Mind is an immaterial information substance.
```

The physically relevant units are constraint-bearing states with measurable causal consequences.

**Status: A (proven — follows from information theory).**

### 2.4 The Material Enactment Principle

A subject-bearing process is not an abstract transition table.

**Definition 3 (Material Regime):** Let Σ_i(t) be the material regime of process A_i:

```
Σ_i = ⟨W_i, I_i, L_i, P_i, C_i, E_i, M_i⟩
```

where W_i is hydration state, I_i ionic composition, L_i lipid membranes, P_i proteins and channels, C_i cytoskeleton, E_i extracellular matrix, M_i metabolism.

**Definition 4 (Materially Enacted Process):**

```
A_i = ⟨X_i, Φ_Σi, H_i, 𝒞_i, 𝒥_i, Ω_i⟩
```

where X_i is the accessible state space, Φ_Σi the materially dependent dynamics, H_i the causally active history, 𝒞_i the organization of mutual constraints, 𝒥_i the identity-relevant invariants, and Ω_i the current operating regime.

**Principle 1 (Material Enactment):**

```
Φ_Σa ≠ Φ_Σb
```

whenever material differences change psychophysically relevant counterfactual capacities.

**Status: B (supported — Levin's demonstration that bioelectric computation depends on specific biophysical parameters: Fields & Levin 2103.17061; Cadinu et al. 2504.00872).**

### 2.5 Organizational Closure

**Definition 5 (Organizational Closure):** A system exhibits organizational closure when its network of mutually dependent constraints collectively maintains the conditions under which those constraints continue to operate.

Let 𝒞_i = {c₁, ..., c_n} be constraint-producing processes. Closure requires:

```
∀c_j ∈ 𝒞_i, ∃S_j ⊆ 𝒞_i \ {c_j} such that S_j ⇝ c_j
```

Collectively:

```
𝒞_i ⇝ 𝒞_i
```

**Status: A (proven — closure theory); B in organisms (experimentally demonstrated).**

### 2.6 Historical Constitution

**Definition 6 (History):**

```
H_i(t) = {z ∈ X_i(t) : z depends on the prior trajectory and alters future transitions}
```

A process is strongly history-sensitive when:

```
P(x_t+Δ | x_t, H_t) ≠ P(x_t+Δ | x_t)
```

**Status: B (confirmed — non-Markovian dynamics are pervasive in biology).**

## 3. The Exclusion Operation

### 3.1 Formal Definition

Let Ω be the space of all possible determinations.

**Definition 7 (Exclusion Operator):** The exclusion operator E_i for process A_i simultaneously carves subject and world:

```
E_i: Ω → (P_i, W_i)
```

where P_i is the subject pole ("I") and W_i is the world pole ("this").

**Constraint 1 (Co-emergence):** P_i and W_i co-emerge through the same operation:

```
¬(∃P_i prior to E_i) ∧ ¬(∃W_i prior to E_i)
```

**Constraint 2 (Partition):** The output is an ordered pair such that E_i determines both poles jointly. No specification of union, intersection, or idempotence is possible without further assumptions about Ω.

**Status: C (supported — textual evidence from ĪPK 1.3.7; Torella reconstruction of Utpaladeva's apoha theory).**

### 3.2 The Five Constraint Dimensions

The exclusion is implemented along five analytically distinguishable dimensions:

| Operator | Excludes | Subject Pole | World Pole |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kalā (agency) | Actions not available | What I can do | What resists |
| Vidyā (knowledge) | Distinctions not accessible | What I know | What is |
| Rāga (desire) | States not fulfilled | What I want | What offers |
| Kāla (time) | Times not present | When I am | Temporal structure |
| Niyati (space/cause) | Locations not reachable | Where I am | Spatial structure |

**Note:** These dimensions are not independent in practice. Knowledge changes action; action changes spatial reach; desire changes attention. They are analytically distinguishable constraint families, not independent physical operators.

**Status: D (plausible analytic framework; independence not established).**

### 3.3 What the Exclusion Operation Does and Does Not Claim

**Does claim:**
- Content X belongs to field D_i because the same operation E_i that determined X as content simultaneously determined D_i as its field
- Subject and world are co-emergent — neither exists prior to the partition

**Does NOT claim:**
- That E_i solves the hard problem (why the partition is manifest)
- That E_i is demonstrated to be the mechanism of consciousness
- That the kañcukas are independent physical quantities
- That Ω has any properties beyond being the domain of the exclusion

## 7. Predictions

**P1:** Models combining physical organization with phenomenal partition constraints should individuate subjects more reliably than single-domain models.

**P2:** Dynamically stable generating partitions should predict phenomenal transitions better than arbitrary clustering.

**P3:** During large content changes, the organization corresponding to ι_i should remain more stable than content-specific neural patterns.

**P4:** Perturbations of explicit ownership should alter o_i without necessarily abolishing ι_i, unity, or phenomenal presence.

**P5:** Many autonomous biological systems should satisfy organizational closure without satisfying complete psychophysical convergence conditions.

**P6:** History-sensitive models should outperform instantaneous models in predicting temporal continuity, familiarity, and subject-boundary changes.

**P7:** Hydration, membrane voltage, synchrony, information quantity, and complexity should each fail as independent selectors while contributing to the joint process model.

**P8:** The candidate bearer should be recovered across a range of nearby spatial and temporal scales rather than at one fragile analytical resolution.

## 8. Falsification Conditions

The framework would be seriously weakened if:

1. No physical model family converges on a common process-equivalence class
2. Stable phenomenal partitions cannot be mapped to stable physical partitions
3. Arbitrary partitions predict phenomenology as well as generating partitions
4. Candidate boundaries change radically under minor analytical alterations
5. Organizational closure and causal-scale robustness provide no predictive improvement
6. Historical variables add no explanatory value
7. Phenomenal unity and privacy track several incompatible overlapping processes equally well
8. Physical destruction of the recovered process leaves the same phenomenal trajectory intact
9. The proposed subject-index invariant shows no physical or phenomenological stability

Hoffman models conscious agents as Markovian kernels with composition rules that reproduce quantum structures as special cases. This provides the most complete formal ontology for a consciousness-first metaphysics. However, it makes the physical world a user interface icon, which contradicts the Material Enactment Principle. The tension remains unresolved.

**Status: E (competing metaphysical framework, not compatible with Ochema's dual-aspect monism).**

### 10.4 Causal Emergence (Hoel 2025)

Hoel's theory treats different scales of a system as slices of a higher-dimensional object. When EI(macro) > EI(micro), the macroscale has unique causal contributions. This provides a formal measure for the "causal-scale robustness" component of bearer recovery. Integration with the present framework is in progress.

**Status: D (mathematically well-defined; application to consciousness is speculative).**

### 10.5 Developmental Aperture Trajectory

Speculative extension: the exclusion profile E_i evolves over developmental and evolutionary time. The "densities" described in some traditions (Ra, Kashmir Śaivism) may correspond to stages of this evolution. No longitudinal data exist.

**Status: E (speculative).**

### 10.6 Transindividual Subjects

Can multiple subjects form a higher-order subject while retaining lower-order individuality? This would require a composition rule for exclusion operators. No such rule is known, and the combination problem remains unsolved.

**Status: E (open problem).**
