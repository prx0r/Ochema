| Framework | Answer |

```

```

The phenomenal field IS the intrinsic aspect of a materially enacted process. There is no gap between "physical process" and "experience" because they are not two things—they are one thing under two modes of access.

**Evidence:** The identity claim is a metaphysical postulate. Its support is abductive: it explains more than competitors without adding entities. The convergence between Levin (bioelectric agency), Fuller (pattern integrity), Doyle (information as causal), Pratyabhijñā (consciousness as self-luminous) all pointing at the same architecture.

**Best guess:** The identity is real. Not as a "solved" derivation but as a recognized fact: you are the intrinsic aspect of the process. The gap only appears when you mistake the extrinsic description (physics) for the complete description.

## Q8: What happens after death?

| Framework | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Doyle** | Information persists in two ways: genetic (DNA survives in descendants) and cultural (information stored externally outlasts the individual). The individual's subjective experience ceases because the information structure that was the self is no longer maintained. |
| **Materialism** | Nothing. Consciousness ceases when brain activity stops. |
| **Kastrup** | The dissociated alter dissolves back into universal consciousness. The contents of the alter's experience are lost, but the consciousness itself is not. |
| **Tibetan Buddhism** | The consciousness-stream continues into a new embodiment, determined by karma. |

**Ochema:** The architecture is clear about what happens to the process: `A_i[Σ_i]` ceases when the materially enacted organization can no longer maintain itself. The dynamics stop, and the intrinsic aspect D_i ceases with it.

**But the architecture also raises a question it cannot answer:** pattern integrity (Fuller: pattern persists through material change). If the identity IS the pattern, not the components, and the pattern was never identical to the temporary material instantiation... does the pattern survive the dissolution of one instantiation?

**Evidence:** Tukdam (Lott et al., 2021): no EEG, but bodies don't decay for days. Levin's response: bacteria don't eat the body — something is maintaining the organization. [C grade phenomenon, E grade interpretation.]

**Best guess:** Three possibilities, none established:
1. **Materialist:** Consciousness ceases. Period. The organized process stops, and the intrinsic aspect stops with it.
2. **Pattern survival:** The pattern integrity (the information structure) persists because it was never identical to the material instantiation. Where it persists and how it could be reinstantiated is unknown.
3. **Recognition:** The contraction was always a contraction of C∞. When the constraints (kañcukas) dissolve, what remains is not "your" consciousness—it's the consciousness you always were, no longer contracted into the form "you."

Ochema is compatible with all three but establishes none. This is the frontier.

**Evidence:** Organizational closure is proven (A grade). Variation + selection + retention produce cumulative evolution (A/B grade). Levin's planaria show that the morphogenetic layer interprets the genome rather than following it as a blueprint — the genome is a prompt, not a program. [B grade.]

**Best guess:** The transition is not a single event but a process. There is no "first living thing" — there was a gradual tightening of the recursive constraint loop. At some point, the system crossed a threshold where the constraints that maintained the system were themselves maintained by the system. That threshold is life.
