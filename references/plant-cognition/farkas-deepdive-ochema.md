## PART I: WHAT FARKAS ET AL. (2025) ACTUALLY SHOWED

### 1.1 The design

- **Organism:** tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum* cv. Micro Tom)
- **Stimuli:** ultrasound (US) and drought (D), alone, combined
  (US+D), and repeated — sampled at 2, 5, 7, 10, 12 days
- **Methods:** mRNA-seq (transcription) + whole-genome bisulfite
  sequencing (WGBS, DNA methylation) + RT-qPCR (target genes)
- **Question:** do plants memorize stimuli? For how long? Do they
  form associations (Pavlovian conditioning)?

### 1.2 The four findings

**Finding 1 — the register changes (2 days):**
Two days after exposure, significantly altered gene transcription
AND DNA methylation. The state ω of the plant — its molecular
register — was rewritten by the stimulus.

**Finding 2 — memory persists (5–10 days):**
Four target genes showed the stimulus was memorized: "plants
memorized stimuli for 5–10 d, in a gene- and stimulus-dependent
way." Memory types:
- **Type 1 (sustained induction):** expression stays elevated
  (hysteresis — the state doesn't return to baseline)
- **Type 2 (modified re-induction):** re-exposure produces a
  different response than the first exposure
- **Habituation:** repeated harmless stimulus → response quiets
  down ("the plants may have learned and memorized to ignore the
  harmless stimulus")

**Finding 3 — repetition behavior:**
Repeated stimuli cause habituation, sustained induction, or
modified re-induction depending on gene and plant age.

**Finding 4 — ASSOCIATIVE LEARNING (the crown jewel):**
> "Plants were able to use one conditioned stimulus as a predictor
> of the other, unconditioned one, after conditioning in the case
> of 3 out of 4 target genes, and used their transcriptional
> memory associatively."

The Pavlovian structure: combined training (US+D) → then the
previously-neutral ultrasound ALONE elicited the drought-response
in 3 of 4 genes. **The plant learned: ultrasound ⇒ drought is
coming. The sound became a predictor.** (Confirms and extends
Bhandawat et al.: sound as UCS triggering heat-response in
Arabidopsis.)

### 1.3 The substrate

- **The memory is molecular:** DNA methylation (the epigenetic
  layer) + transcriptional state (the expression layer)
- **Data deposited:** GEO GSE303939 (replicable)
- **Peer-reviewed, CC-BY** — this is not fringe science; it's the
  leading edge of plant cognition research, published in a
  standard journal

### 1.4 The replication debate (honest context)

The Gagliano pea-conditioning study (2016) faced a Markel
replication attempt that failed; Gagliano responded that
replication requires identical conditions; Markel countered that
setup differences can't explain absence of effect. Cvrčková &
Konrádová: Markel was testing *reproducibility*, not *replicability*.
**The honest position:** plant associative learning at the
transcriptional level now has TWO independent studies (Bhandawat,
Farkas), plus the behavioral studies (Gagliano Mimosa + pea), plus
the acoustic studies (Khait, Milburn-Johnson). The convergence is
real, though the field remains small.

### 3.4 The testable residue

1. **The habituation-as-precision-weighting test:** if habituation
   IS the orchestration law's downweighting, then the plant's
   response should scale with prediction error — a plant exposed to
   IRREGULAR ultrasound should show stronger responses than one
   exposed to regular (predictable) ultrasound. **Directly testable,
   falsifiable, cheap.**
2. **The icaro test, molecularized:** play the Shipibo icaros to a
   tomato (or the Farkas setup: regular vs irregular ultrasound)
   and measure the methylome. If "the song aligns the body" has a
   mechanism, it's this.
3. **The transgenerational test:** Zhang showed stress-memory passes
   to progeny. Does CONDITIONED memory (the US⇒D association) also
   pass? If the association itself is inherited, the plant's ι_i
   literally carries learned content across generations.
