## 1. THE ROOT VERSES (the strongest evidence — GRETIL-verified)

### TĀ 1/53 — camatkāra as the field of all appearing
(Vol 1:13875–13905 · GRETIL:202)

> "avastutāpi bhāvānāṁ camatkāraikagocarā | yatkuḍyasadṛśī neyaṁ
> dhīravastvetadityapi" — "The non-existence (avastutā) of entities
> is also in one (and the same) field of the wonder (of
> consciousness as is their existence). The notion 'this is
> non-existent (avastu)' is not similar to (an inert object such
> as) a wall."

**Context:** the prakāśa argument (TĀ 1/52–56). Even NEGATION
appears only inside the camatkāra-field. Wonder is the a priori
field of all appearing — existence and non-existence alike. The
epistemological ground of recognition. Dyczkowski's note 340
(Vol 1:13903): "'Wonder' (camatkāra) is the primary characteristic
of consciousness; it is indicative of its sentience, which
distinguishes it from the insentient (jaḍa)."

### TĀ 3/208cd–210ab — the universe is the vibration of the rasa of bliss
(Vol 2:22680–22700 · GRETIL:1822–1828)

> "visargaśaktir yā śaṁbhoḥ setthaṁ sarvatra vartate | tata eva
> samasto'yam ānandarasavibhramaḥ | tathāhi madhure gīte sparśe vā
> candanādike | mādhyasthyavigame yāsau hṛdaye spandamānatā |
> ānandaśaktiḥ saivoktā yataḥ sahṛdayo janaḥ" — "The emission-power
> (visargaśakti) of Śambhu abides thus everywhere. Hence, indeed,
> this entire (universe) is a vibration of the rasa of bliss. For
> example: the vibration in the Heart one experiences while hearing
> a sweet song, feeling a gentle caress, or smelling fragrant
> incense, when the state of indifference towards them ceases, is
> said to be the power of bliss (ānandaśakti), due to which a
> person is sensitive to beauty (sahṛdaya)."

**Context:** TĀ 3 = the Supreme Means (Śāmbhavopāya), the
pratibimbavāda section on visarga (emission) and parāmarśa
(3/201–215). **The emission-power = the rasa of bliss — the
sahṛdaya hears the universe's self-vibration.**

### TĀ 3/229–230ab — aesthetic experience as the arousal of the vitality of consciousness
(Vol 2:24240–24520 · GRETIL:1885–1888)

> "śabdo 'pi madhuro yasmād vīryopacayakārakaḥ | tad dhi vīryaṁ
> paraṁ śuddhaṁ visisṛkṣātmakaṁ matam" — "Even a sweet sound
> intensifies the vitality (vīrya) of consciousness... its nature,
> the desire to emit (visisṛkṣā), is considered to be supremely
> pure." … "tad balaṁ ca tad ojāś ca te prāṇāḥ sā ca kāntatā" —
> "That (vitality) is strength (bala), vigour (ojas), the vital
> breath (prāṇa) and beauty (kāntatā)."

**Jayaratha's commentary (Vol 2:24380–24410) — the fullest
aesthetic theory in the TĀ corpus:** "To the degree in which this
vitality does not nourish (consciousness), the wonder (of the
experience of beauty) decreases. A total absence of wonder is
insentience itself. (Conversely,) aesthetic sensitivity
(sahṛdayatā) is (a state of) penetration into a heightened (state
of) wonder... this same wonder even when suffering. **The nature
of suffering is, essentially, a particular kind of wonder
(camatkriyā).**" Dyczkowski's note (Vol 2:24400): "by discussing
the processes at work in aesthetic experience here, Abhinava
indicates that it is part of the Divine Means (śāmbhavopāya)...
the direct experience of consciousness as the 'I', which
dynamically engulfs objectivity and incorporates it into itself."

### TĀ 3/240cd–241ab — sahṛdaya vs. ahṛdaya
(Vol 2:25420–25460 · GRETIL:1918)

> "yeṣāṁ na tanmayībhūtis te dehādinimajjanam ... ahṛdayā iti" —
> "Those who do not experience the state of oneness (with the
> beautiful object) and the submergence of their identification
> with the body etc., are considered to be insensitive to beauty
> (ahṛdaya)."

### TĀ 4/115 — the universe is saturated with the rasa of self-awareness
(Vol 3:9660–9760 · GRETIL:2425)

> "viśvam etat svasaṁvittirasanirbharitaṁ rasāt" — "(The yogi,)
> having penetrated (spontaneously) by virtue of (his) aesthetic
> delight (rasāt) into this universe, full to overflowing with the
> aesthetic sentiment (rasa) of his own consciousness, (he) should
> offer libation (tarpayet) to the entire circle of the Paths."

**Context:** TĀ 4 = the Empowered Means (Śāktopāya) — the higher
nondual worship (Sanderson 1992: 297–298: the source of the
Bhairava-tantra nondual pūjā). Jayaratha ad 4/115 (Vol 3:9769): the
yogi's penetration "by virtue of the fullness of the wonder
(camatkāra) of his own Self... full to overflowing with the
aesthetic sentiment (rasa) of his own consciousness." **Root-verse
ontology: the world IS the rasa of consciousness.**

### TĀ 4/171cd–172ab — everything as one relish (ekarasa)
(Vol 3:17370–17400 · GRETIL:2592)

Root: "meyaugha iti yatsarvamatra cinmātrameva tat |
svācchandyamanapekṣaṃ yatsā parā parameśvarī" — "All this — the
object, means of knowledge, subject and knowledge — ... is (pure)
consciousness alone (cinmātra)."

Jayaratha's gloss (Vol 3:17388): "**The meaning is that it
manifests as one aesthetic relish (ekarasa).** That therefore is
the 'independence' (of one's own) self-luminous and supreme
'consciousness'." — the ekarasa gloss is Jayaratha's; the root
asserts cinmātra as svācchandya (freedom).

### TĀ 5/43–52ab — the Six Blisses (ānandāḥ)
(Vol 4:3192–3900 · GRETIL:3002ff)

The blisses are a gradient of self-recognition through the five
breaths (āṇavopāya — "Utterance through the Principle of the Vital
Breath"):

1. **Nijānanda** (Innate Bliss) — pure subjective aspect, lucid
   deep sleep in the Heart (base state)
2. **Nirānanda** (Bliss of Stillness) — exhalation, means of
   knowledge
3. **Parānanda** (Bliss of the Other) — inhalation, the object of
   knowledge
4. **Brahmānanda** (Bliss of the Brahman) — equal breath (samāna),
   equality of all objects — **added in the TĀ** (not in the MVV)
5. **Mahānanda** (Great Bliss) — rising breath (udāna) — "the
   state which is Great Bliss arises from the means of knowledge
   that is associated with act of relishing (objectivity)
   (carvaṇā)" (Vol 4:3358)
6. **Cidānanda** (Bliss of Consciousness) — pervasive breath
   (vyāna), "not augmented with any insentient thing"
7. **Jagadānanda** (Cosmic Bliss) — the sixth beyond the five:
   "where there is no limitation at all... fed by the Supreme
   Nectar (of freedom), wherein meditation (bhāvanā) or the like
   finds no direct application" — Dyczkowski: "the highest plane
   of bliss is **Anupāya — No Means**." Taught to Abhinava by
   Śambhunātha (Vol 4:3867, 3900). The pentadic MVV prototype is
   tied to the five faces of Sadāśiva (Vol 4:3260–3340).

### TĀ 6/80cd–81ab, 84cd–85ab — carvaṇa: the "setting" of consciousness
(Vol 4:21328, 21532 · GRETIL:3639, 3649)

> "yāvān evodayo vitter vedyaikagrahatatparaḥ | tāvad evāstamayanaṁ
> veditṛ svātmacarvaṇam" — "To the degree in which the dawning of
> consciousness takes place, such is its setting, **which is the
> perceiver (who) is the (blissful) relish (carvaṇa) of its own
> nature**."

> "kadācid vastuviśrāntisāmyenātmani carvaṇam || vedyavedakasāmyaṁ
> tat sā rātridinatulyatā" — "Sometimes the repose in outer
> reality and the relish (of consciousness) within oneself are
> equally long. This equality of the knower and the known is
> equivalent to the equality of Night and Day."

**Context:** TĀ 6 — the ahorātra practice. Carvaṇa is the
technical name for the subjective phase of each cognitive "day":
perception dawns outward (day), relishes the self (night).

### TĀ 11/63, 76cd–79ab — camatkāratāratamya: the gradation of wonder
(Vol 7:5480–5700 · GRETIL:7760–7820)

> 11/77: "yathā yathā cākṛtakaṁ tadrūpam atiricyate | tathā tathā
> camatkāratāratamyaṁ vibhāvyate" — "**One experiences
> progressively higher degrees of the aesthetic delight
> (camatkāra) to the degree in which that uncreated form (of
> consciousness) becomes prominent.**"
> 11/78–79: those established in creative intuition (pratibhā)
> "certainly attain eloquence and poetic genius (kavitva)."

**Context:** TĀ 11 = the kalādhvan, the phoneme/mantra section.
Camatkāra is GRADED (tāratamya) — its grades are the degrees of
awakening of pratibhā. Dyczkowski's note (Vol 7:5602–5605): "The
concept of camatkāra is absent in the nondualist Śaiva philosophy
of Somānanda. Accordingly, **Gnoli surmises that it was probably
introduced into the context of philosophical discourse by his
disciple, Utpaladeva.**"

### TĀ 13/43cd–44ab — wonder firm without objects
(Vol 3:35060–35080)

> "sthairyam eti camatkāro vinā viṣayasaṅgatim" — "Once having
> entered within (the essentially conscious nature), the state of
> wonder (camatkāra) becomes firm without (needing to) associate
> with (outer) objects."

### TĀ 26/61–65 — the inner-worship hymn: the world as many-flavoured juice
(Vol 9:24240–24360 · GRETIL:13610–13630) — **the strongest root
rasa-ontology**

> 26/61cd: "śivābhedabharād bhāvavargaḥ ścyotati yaṁ rasam" — "the
> juice (of aesthetic delight) (rasa) **that exudes from (the
> multitude of) phenomena, (pressed down) because of the weight of
> (their) oneness with Śiva**."
> 26/64: "kṛtvādhāradharāṁ camatkṛtirasaprokṣākṣaṇakṣālitāṁ...
> tvāṁ devyā saha dehadevasadane devārcaye 'harniśam" — "I worship
> You... day and night having washed the altar of its foundation
> with the spray of the essence (rasa) of (my) aesthetic rapture
> (camatkṛtirasa)."
> 26/65: "nānāsvādarasām imāṁ trijagatīṁ hṛccakrayantrārpitām...
> niṣpīḍya niḥṣyanditam | yat saṁvitparamāmṛtaṁ... tena tvāṁ
> haviṣā pareṇa parame saṁtarpaye 'harniśam" — "**I place this
> triple world, which is the juice (of aesthetic delight of) many
> flavours (nānāsvādarasa), on the machine of the Wheel of the
> Heart. Pressing (down upon it) with the great weight of
> discrimination... the supreme nectar of consciousness that ends
> birth, old age and death flows (gushing from it).**"

**Context:** TĀ 26 — the daily ritual. Ontology: the world is
multi-flavoured rasa; consciousness is the press that extracts from
it the supreme nectar. (Verse 64 repeated verbatim at TĀ 29/176.)

### TĀ 28/373cd–378ab — the melaka: contraction, mutual reflection, and the dance
(Vol 10:11090–11280) — **the intersubjective aesthetics core**

> 28/373cd–374ab: "saṁvit sarvātmikā dehabhedād yā saṅkucet tu sā |
> melake 'nyonyasaṅghaṭṭapratibimbād vikasvarā" — "**The
> consciousness, which is all things, that contracts due to the
> differences (bheda) between the bodies, returns to its state of
> expansion during a gathering (melaka), because of the (mutual)
> reflection (pratibimba) of their consciousness by (their) union
> with one another.**"
> 28/375cd–376ab: "ata eva gītagītaprabhṛtau bahuparṣadi | yaḥ
> sarvatanmayībhāve hlādo na tv ekakasya saḥ" — "Thus, in a
> gathering of many people (for a performance) of song, dance and
> the like, **the enjoyment (they experience is collective) when
> they are all one (equally immersed in the show together), (that
> joy) is not (experienced by) one (who is alone).**"
> 28/376cd–377ab: "ānandanirbharā saṁvit pratyekaṁ sā tathaikatām |
> nṛttādau viṣaye prāptā pūrṇānandatvam aśnute" — "**The
> consciousness, full to overflowing with bliss, (of each one)
> individually, attaining in this way oneness when dance and the
> like is its object, enjoys a state of complete bliss.**"
> 28/377cd–378ab: "īrṣyāsūyādisaṅkocakāraṇābhāvato 'tra sā |
> vikasvarā niṣpratighaṁ saṁvid ānandayoginī" — "It is because the
> causes of the contraction, such as jealousy, envy and the like,
> are absent, that consciousness is here expanding without
> obstacles."

**Context:** TĀ 28 — the Kaula assembly (yoginīmelaka). Dyczkowski
(Vol 10:11130–11150): the participants are "like mirrors placed in
front of each other that reflect repeatedly to form an infinite
series of reflections, the contracted consciousness of the Siddhas
and Yoginīs goes on expanding." **The shared aesthetic event is the
mechanism of de-contraction.** (The dance passage — Vol 10:11233–
11235.)

### TĀ 29/61, 29/136cd–138ab — carvaṇa in the root text
(Vol 10:19287, 19358, 21540–21600 · GRETIL:15550)

> 29/61: "bhairavaikacamatkāracarvaṇaikasvarāpiṇīm" — (the Dūtī)
> whose "sole nature is the relish (carvaṇa) of nothing but
> Bhairava's unique aesthetic rapture (camatkāra)."
> 29/137cd–138ab: "raṇaraṇakarasān nijarasabharitabahirbhāva-
> carvaṇavaśena | viśrāntidhāma kiñcil labdhvā svātmanyathārpayate"
> — "**By savouring (carvaṇa) outer entities filled with (the
> sensory deities') own aesthetic savour (rasa)**, ... the host of
> deities attains to the ineffable abode of rest and places those
> outer entities within its own essential (conscious) nature."

**Context:** TĀ 29 — the Kaula yāga; the return of the senses to
the Heart. The only root use of carvaṇa as a technical term:
perception completes itself by relishing its object as one's own
rasa.

### TĀ 32/18, 32/37cd–40ab — rasa-in-rasa
(Vol 11:9824, 10255)

> 32/18: "śūlaṁ samarasaṁ kṛtvā rase rasa iva sthitaḥ" — "made
> the Trident one, (the yogi) abides like the juice (of aesthetic
> delight) (rasa) (merged) into juice."

**Context:** TĀ 32 — union of the breaths/Kuṇḍalinī; rasa-oneness
as the description of samādhi.

### TĀ 36/15 — the Tantrāloka oozes rasas
(Vol 11:14708–14712 · GRETIL:17373)

> "adhyuṣṭasaṁtatisrotaḥsārabhūtarasāhṛtim | vidhāya tantrāloko
> 'yaṁ syandate sakalānrasān" — "**Having extracted the juice
> (rasa) which is the essence of the current of (these) three and
> a half lineages, this (our work), the Tantrāloka, oozes with
> all the juices (rasa) (of aesthetic sentiment).**"

Dyczkowski's note (Vol 1:5739–5745): "Śiva consciousness, the one
reality, is not only to be known – realised – **it must also be
experienced to be beautiful, indeed as Beauty itself.** The
experience of metaphysical Knowledge is insight and certainty, the
experience of Beauty is sentiment. While the model of the former
is light and the sense of sight, the model of the latter is taste
and the sense of taste. We 'savour' or 'relish' Beauty, just as
we 'see' Reality."

### TĀ 1/116cd–122ab — rasa as nourishing juice (the literal etymology)
(Vol 1:21430–21680)

> 1/119: "ye ca svabhāvato varṇā rasaniḥṣyandino yathā" — "Those
> letters from which, by their (very) nature, (this) juice (rasa)
> flows..."

Dyczkowski's note (Vol 1:21639–21655): "'Rasa' means, most
literally, 'juice'... chyle... semen... **The Upaniṣads mean to say
that the Brahman is the vital essence of all things, when they
declare that He is rasa (raso vai saḥ — Taittirīya Upaniṣad
2/7/1).** Vitalizing and transformative, the alchemical elixir is
also called rasa." "Dryness" (śuṣka) = absence of rasa.

## 2. THE COMMENTARIAL MATERIAL (authentic, cited — not root)

### PTv p. 86–87 — camatkāra as "my-ness"
(Vol 1:1198–1228; the COMPENDIUM's "TĀ 1 1262–1270" — actual lines
1198–1228)

> "'My' (mama) is the Heart, which is the place that is the abode
> of the foundation of all things... Thus the 'my' (mama) is the
> perceiver (pramātṛ) from which develops the (inscrutable) aspect
> of (his consciousness, which is) the uninterrupted (and
> unconditioned) wonder (of its aesthetic relish) (camatkāra) (as
> the experience) 'this appears to me (mama)'... This is 'my'
> Heart of all the endless number of things such as (the colour)
> blue. The Void (of pure consciousness) (vyoman) (in the Heart)
> is where the differentiated universe, which is wonder, is
> properly sustained."

**Context:** TĀv ad 1/68–69 — the Heart as the Deity invoked at
the TĀ's opening. Ownership ("my") = aesthetic wonder = the
perceiver.

### PTv p. 19 — "the aesthetic relish of consciousness"
(Vol 1:1414–1420; the COMPENDIUM's "TĀ 1 1457–1459" — actual
1414–1420)

> "This is not the case with supreme consciousness itself, the
> supreme Bhairava, which is perfectly full, because **it is
> always by its very nature essentially the aesthetic relish of
> consciousness which is independent of all things and most
> free.** (PTv p. 19)."

The term is defined at length in the PTv (p. 19–28) — **sixteen
meanings of Anuttara, of which this is the first.** Immediately
following (Vol 1:1445–1448): "It is the ground of the power of
consciousness (cicchakti) **which wields the Trident of the three
energies of will, knowledge and action.**"

### Torella on ĪP 1/5/11 — the gloss-cluster of camatkāra
(Vol 1:2279–2293)

> Utpaladeva's vṛtti: objects that appear within consciousness,
> unlike a crystal, evoke emotive, aesthetic responses within it.
> **They are 'savoured' by it. The word he uses here is
> 'camatkṛti'.**
> Torella: "Abhinava (IPVV vol. 2 p. 117) says: camatkṛti means
> the act of a person savouring (bhuñjanasya), that is, the bliss
> constituted by the full achievement of fruition... The state of
> 'wonder' (camatkṛti, camatkāra) in its highest form is thus
> both the act of savouring the aesthetic object and the
> plenitude, absolute independence of consciousness devoid of
> obstacles (avighnā saṁvit), the savouring of which is in the
> final analysis **a savouring of one's own self and one's own
> bliss**... The terms with which it is glossed may be grouped
> according to its principal components: cognition, bliss, and
> wonder. The first group includes... **vimarśa (pratyavamarśa
> etc), carvaṇā, āsvāda and rasanā**; the second includes
> **ānanda, nivṛti, viśrānti, laya**; the third **vismaya,
> vikāsa.**"

**Correction to the project files:** the "three-register model"
is Torella's classification — not merely "our reconstruction,"
and certainly not Abhinava's own grouping.

### The music passage — sound, darśana, madhura-rasa
(ad TĀ 3/209–210, Vol 2:22850–22890)

- MVV 1/897–898ab: the supreme radiant energy "aroused by (the
  beauty of) the season, flowers..., enters into a state of
  emission, by virtue of which it is engaged in the act (of
  savouring) the wonder of bliss."
- IPVv 2, p. 178–179: the pleasure of poetry/drama "differs from
  the pleasure derived from sense objects... the experience is
  one of 'relishing' (rasanā), 'tasting' (carvaṇa), or
  'contentment' (nivṛti), and is, in fact, **repose in the
  cognizing subjectivity.**"
- Śivopādhyāya on VBh 73: "the savouring of the objects of sense,
  such as sound... is the sense of the wonder that that brings it
  about. In the same way, **there is the wonder (aroused) by
  seeing something that possesses a very beautiful form
  (darśanacamatkāraḥ).** Such too is the savour of the relish of
  a sweet juice and the like."
- **The vīṇa passage** (MVV 1/180–182; Vol 1:27216–27220): "A
  person who (is) at one (with the music) (tanmaya) experiences
  the beauty (vaicitrya) in a large number of notes... In this
  doctrine of nonduality this is established without effort:
  even when duality appears, reality is nondual."
- Note ad 3/209–210 (Vol 2:22710–22740): "**The concepts of bliss
  (ānanda), savour of aesthetic sentiment (rasāsvāda) and
  emission (viṣarga) are fundamentally the same.**... Sweet song,
  a pleasing picture... are full of a 'juice' (rasa), which the
  senses relish or 'taste', and which, like food, feeds
  consciousness, revealing the innate delight and wonder
  (camatkāra) which is its own reflective awareness."

### Anupāya = the power of bliss
(Vol 1:30748–30755)

> Bliss (ānanda) is the inner nature of the Absolute."

## 4. THE KEY DOCTRINAL MOVES

1. **Rasa as the essence of consciousness** — PTv p. 19 (cited Vol
   1:1417–1419); root variants: TĀ 4/115, 26/61–65, 3/208cd,
   Jayaratha's ekarasa (ad 4/171–172), ahaṅcamatkāra,
   svātmacamatkāramātra.
2. **The five/six blisses as a gradient of self-recognition** —
   tied to the five breaths (five faces of Sadāśiva in the MVV);
   Brahmānanda is a TĀ addition; Mahānanda arises from carvaṇā;
   Jagadānanda = Anupāya, beyond all means. Possible Buddhist
   inspiration (four blisses in Hevajra — Dyczkowski's note).
3. **Camatkāra as "my-ness"** — PTv p. 86–87: the perceiver is
   the "my" from which the uninterrupted wonder develops; root
   correlate TĀ 1/53.
4. **Rasa and the three powers** — the rasa-citation is the ground
   of cicchakti "which wields the Trident of will, knowledge and
   action" (Vol 1:1445–1448); IP 4/6: **kriyā's essence is
   camatkāra**; visargaśakti = ānandarasavibhrama (3/208).
5. **Rasa and pratyabhijñā** — TĀ 1/53 grounds the prakāśa-
   argument in the camatkāra-field; ĪP 1/5/11 (objects are
   "savoured" — recognition's self-relish as the SUPRA-aesthetic
   case); ĪP 1/5/1 (the dance unifying spectators); ahaṅcamatkāra
   as the recognition-formula. Gnoli: camatkāra entered
   philosophical discourse with **Utpaladeva**.
6. **Rasa in the upāyas** — Śāmbhavopāya (TĀ 3: the aesthetic as
   the Divine Means); Śāktopāya (TĀ 4/115, 4/171–172 ekarasa, 6/80–
   85 svātmacarvaṇa); Āṇavopāya (TĀ 5: the Six Blisses as breath-
   practice); Anupāya (Vol 1:30748; TĀ 5/50–52ab Jagadānanda).
7. **The collective aesthetic** — TĀ 28/373–378 (the melaka:
   mutual reflection = de-contraction); TĀ 10/83–88 (the shared
   spectacle; "arid" without the audience); ĪP 1/5/1 (the dance
   unifying the spectators). **The shared aesthetic event is the
   mechanism of de-contraction — intersubjective coherence
   raising.**
8. **The orgasm = visargaśakti** — Vol 10:21120: "the initial
   moment of orgasm as well as emissive power of consciousness
   about to pour out into creation"; Vol 10:20551: visarga = the
   ejaculation marking the climax of orgasm. **Pleasure IS
   creation; the emission IS the state's overflow.**

## 5. THE SCHOLARSHIP (what the field says)

### Timalsina 2007, "Metaphor, Rasa, and Dhvani" (Method and Theory in the Study of Religion 19)
- Rasa and dhvani as hermeneutic tools for the Tantric corpus;
  the abhivyakti (revelation) theory: rasa is dormant, revealed
  when causal conditions are met — "just as the self is revealed
  in self-recognition."
- Jagannātha: rasa = cidāvaraṇabhaṅga — "the removal of the
  cover to consciousness," letting the individual be in his true
  nature, "awareness-in-itself."
- Maheśvarānanda: rasa = the very Absolute, following "raso vai
  saḥ" — the rasas are rasa only in the upacāra (accessory)
  sense.
- Camatkāra: "the veil that hides the essential nature of
  awareness is removed, and the 'real' self is directly felt in
  the domain where the two modes of subjective and objective
  awareness are united in one camatkāra, or the ah hah!-moment."
- The four theories of rasa-niṣpatti: utpattivāda (Lollaṭa:
  origination), anumitivāda (Śaṅkuka: inference), bhuktivāda
  (Bhaṭṭa Nāyaka: consumption), **abhivyaktivāda
  (Ānandavardhana & Abhinavagupta: revelation).**
- The seven blisses (TĀ 5.44–51) culminating in jagadānanda;
  "All experiences of bliss are the very experience of the self";
  "ānando brahmaṇo rūpaṃ tac ca dehe vyavasthitam" — bliss is the
  form of Brahman, located in the body.
- Utpala's parable: the self is the unrecognized lover who cannot
  "taste his own divine grandeur, just because he does not
  recognize himself."
- "Aesthetic and mystical... do not pose a distinction but rather
  function as different dimensions."

### Timalsina 2021, "Rasāsvāda: A Comparative Approach to Emotion and the Self" (JIPR 26)
- Rasa is not the surge of raw emotion — a refined, reflexive,
  transcendent savoring (lokottara); dormant emotion + vibhāva
  transforms into rasa.
- **Śānta as the mahārasa**: "the very experience of the self
  (svātmarūpa)"; "all feelings of aesthetic experience emerge
  from śānta rasa and eventually return to it"; "what we
  consider as the self is the very emotion in its most exalted
  state"; "To feel peace is not to have an absence of all other
  emotions... This, to Abhinava, is when the self returns to the
  self."
- "there is no distinction between rasa and the self"; rasa "is
  equated with the self, of the character of bliss (ānanda), it
  is fundamental to all experience. Rasa, therefore, is not a
  product. It is just revealed when the perfect somatic and
  psychological conditions present themselves."
- Rasa is karmically neutral — "does not constitute a karmic
  complex" — only possible "when the rasa experience is viewed as
  identical to the exposure to the self."
- Carvaṇā: aesthetic savoring as "chewing" — transcendent rasa,
  embodied mechanism; Mammaṭa's paan-chewing image.
- Rasa neither nirvikalpaka nor savikalpaka — "of the character
  of both" (Abhinavagupta).
- Rasa vs yogic absorption: differ "only in the sense that rasa
  is transitory while yogic experience can be permanent."
- Rasa as the "intermediate paradigm" between cognitive and
  perceptual theories of emotion.

### Lidke 2011, "The Resounding Field of Visualised Self-Awareness" (JHS 4)
- Synesthesia as a bi-directional hermeneutic for the Śrī Yantra
  rituals: the yantra as visual image + resounding sonic field of
  graphemes; ritual as a technology for generating "embodied
  poly-sensualised consciousness" whose fruit is recognition of
  one's self as non-distinct from the goddess.
- Rasa peripheral; the aesthetic-religious register is shared.

## 7. WHAT THE TEXT ACTUALLY SUPPORTS (the graded bottom line)

1. **Root, A-grade textual:** the universe IS the rasa of
   consciousness (4/115, 26/61–65, 3/208cd); camatkāra is the
   field of all appearing, existence and non-existence (1/53);
   camatkāra is graded and its grades are the awakening of
   pratibhā (11/77); the collective aesthetic event de-contracts
   consciousness (28/373–378, 10/86–88); carvaṇa completes
   perception (6/80–85, 29/136–138); the blisses are a gradient
   of self-recognition through the breaths (5/43–52).
2. **Commentarial, authentic (PTv etc.):** consciousness IS
   aesthetic relish (PTv 19); camatkāra = my-ness (PTv 86–87);
   kriyā's essence is camatkāra (IP 4/6); orgasm = visargaśakti
   (Vol 10:21120).
3. **Scholarly, corroborating:** rasa = the self's own experience
   (Timalsina); śānta = the mahārasa; rasa karmically neutral;
   rasa revealed, not produced (abhivyakti).
4. **Not in the text:** sādhāraṇīkaraṇa, rasa-rūpatā, the
   three-register model as Abhinava's own, ahaṅcamatkāra as root.

**The keystone verse for the ochema connection: TĀ 26/65 — the
triple world as multi-flavoured juice pressed by consciousness into
the supreme nectar.** The world is the state's own rasa; the
practice is the pressing; the nectar is the coherence extracted.
