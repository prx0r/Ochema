## PART I: WHO HE WAS (the biography that matters)

**Viktor Stepanovich Grebennikov (1927–2001), Novosibirsk.**

- **Real entomologist and apiology specialist** — self-taught (no
  higher education), but a genuine naturalist, animal artist, and
  bee researcher
- **GULAG survivor:** sentenced 1947 to 20 years for forging bread
  cards (drew them by hand); nearly died of starvation; saved by
  his drawing talent (made propaganda posters); released by amnesty
  1953
- Founded the Museum of Agroecology and Environmental Protection
  (Krasnoobsk, 1976)
- Honored Ecologist of Russia; member of the International
  Association of Bee Researchers
- Published real entomology books: *A Million Riddles* (1968), *In
  the Land of Insects* (1979), *Secrets of the World of Insects*
  (1990) — these are legitimate popular-science entomology
- The paranormal claims came in his self-published *My World*
  (1997/98)

**The framing that matters:** he was a REAL scientist — an
observer, a drawer, a naturalist with genuine field credentials —
who crossed the evidence boundary late in life. The pattern is
familiar: Eskridge, Backster, Puthoff — the credentialed technician
whose aperture softened at the boundary of the known.

## PART II: THE CLAIMS (verbatim from the canonical document)

### 2.1 The discovery (1988)

> "In 1988 he found out antigravitational effects of chitinous
> covers of some insects species."

### 2.2 The attendant phenomenon (invisibility)

> "But the most astonishing attendant phenomenon associated with
> antigravity was a phenomenon overall either partial invisibility
> or deformed visual perception of the material object which was
> located in a zone of compensated gravitation."

### 2.3 The platform

> "Based on this opening and by using bionics principles, the
> author designed and built an antigravitational platform, and
> also, practically, developed principles of manned flight with the
> speed up to 25 km/min. Since 1991–92 the device was used by the
> author as a means of fast movement."

### 2.4 The general biological law (the theoretical claim)

Cherednichenko's framing — biogravitation as a general law:

> "It would be a mistake to think that similar opportunities are
> granted only to naturally gifted people. On my belief it's a
> general biological law which is insufficiently investigated."

Supported by: somnambulist weight reduction, epileptic "superhuman
strength," athletes' performance under psychological training —
the claim that biogravitational mechanisms are advanced in
sportsmen.

### 2.5 The CSE (the mechanism claim)

The "Cavity Structural Effect": hollow, periodic structures
(beehive cells, insect chitin covers, honeycombs) produce field
effects — the 1985 priority claim with V.F. Zolotarev:

> "The phenomenon of interaction of multi-cavity structures with
> living systems" — priority certificate № 32-OT-11170,
> 03.09.1985

And the 1984 publications:
- "On the physico-biological properties of pollinator-bee
  nestings" (*Siberian Bulletin of Agricultural Science*, 1984)
- "The Secret of the Beehive" (*Tekhnika–Molodyozhi*, 1984 № 6)
- "The Secret of Solitary Bee Nestings" (*Pchelovodstvo*, 1984
  № 12)

### 2.6 The patent (1995)

A Russian patent on a device with beehive cells (dry honeycomb)
claimed to enhance the effectiveness of therapeutic drugs — the
most modest and testable form of the claim.

## PART III: THE HONEST VERDICT — IS IT REAL?

### 3.1 The failures (why it's not accepted science)

| Failure | Detail |
|---|---|
| **No replication** | Nobody else has reproduced CSE — no independent lab, no controlled demonstration |
| **No mechanism** | Chitin is a polysaccharide; honeycomb is a periodic structure; there is NO known coupling between periodic hollow structures and gravity. The claim contradicts established physics with no mechanism offered |
| **No public demonstration** | The platform flew only in his accounts; "the camera shutter jammed due to a time-warping force-field" is the classic unfalsifiable escape |
| **The April Fools' detail** | The gravitolyot was described in the **April 1 (April Fools') issue of Tekhnika–Molodyozhi (1993-04, p.44)** — the magazine's joke issue. The "levitation platform" claim entered public discourse through a humor publication |
| **Self-published** | *My World* was self-published (Sovetskaya Sibir, 1997), outside peer review |
| **The mixed record** | His entomology books are respected; the CSE is not — same man, two registers (the thesis's exact prediction) |

**The verdict: the CSE is Tier-4 — unverified, unreplicated,
mechanism-less, and contaminated by the April Fools' publication.**
The "antigravity platform" claim is as strong as a claim can be
while being entirely without evidence.

### 3.2 What IS real (separating the registers)

1. **The entomology is real** — his observations of insect
   behavior, his drawings, his beekeeping research were genuine
2. **The observation instinct was real** — he noticed something
   (odd sensations near beehives, anomalous instrument readings)
   — the question is what
3. **The 1984 papers and 1985 priority claim were real
   submissions** — he did formally claim the "discovery" (the
   priority certificate exists)
4. **The patent (1995) is real** — a modest, testable claim about
   honeycomb enhancing drug delivery

**The honest question:** was he a fraud, deluded, or a genuine
observer of a misread phenomenon? The evidence supports: a real
naturalist who experienced SOMETHING (possibly electrostatic,
possibly the body's own response to structured environments,
possibly ordinary placebo/hypnosis effects — the somnambulist
parallels he drew) and interpreted it through the only framework
available to him: a heroic, GULAG-survivor's cosmology of hidden
natural powers.

### 4.2 The two-register reading (the thesis's exact prediction)

> **The same bearer, two registers: the entomologist who wrote real
> field guides and the mystic who flew over Siberia at 25 km/min.
> The engineer's exclusions (what counts as evidence) were tight
> in the field — insect taxonomy, observation, drawing — and
> softened at the boundary of the known. The aperture changed; the

The thesis doesn't need CSE to be real. It needs to explain how a

### 4.3 The genuinely interesting residue (the only experiment worth doing)

The 1995 patent is the one testable piece: **does dry honeycomb
(cellular, periodic, chitin-wax structure) measurably modulate a
biological response — drug delivery, cell culture, plant
germination?** This is testable, cheap, and falsifiable. If
honeycomb does nothing (expected), the CSE dies cleanly. If it does
SOMETHING (e.g., a structured-environment effect on germination
rates — plausible via humidity/electrostatic/mechanical factors,
NOT gravity), then the claim's residue is real but the mechanism is
mundane. **This is the honest experiment: kill the magic, test the
structure.**

## PART V: THE VERDICT

**Grebennikov was a real entomologist who made an unreplicable
claim.** The science of CSE: no mechanism, no replication, no
demonstration, and the platform entered culture through an April
Fools' issue. **As stated, the CSE is almost certainly false — and
the thesis says so at full strength (Tier-4, no evidence).**

**What the thesis keeps:**
1. The **pattern** — another credentialed observer crossing the
   boundary (Eskridge, Backster, Puthoff, Grebennikov: the
   scientist-claimer family)
2. The **structure** — periodic structured environments × living
   systems is a real research line (the coherent-domain/sound work),
   and the 1995 patent makes it testable
3. The **discipline** — the April Fools' detail is why grading
   matters: a claim that entered through a humor publication is a
   claim on probation from birth

**The one-line answer: Grebennikov's entomology is real, his
antigravity is almost certainly not, the pattern of the
credentialed-claimer confirms the two-register model, and the only
experiment worth running is the 1995 patent's honeycomb-bioeffect —
which would kill the magic or find the mundane mechanism, either
way honestly.**
