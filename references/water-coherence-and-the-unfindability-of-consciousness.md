## PART II: WHY CONSCIOUSNESS IS UNFINDABLE (the first-person argument)

**The user's insight — and it's the deepest thing in the whole
framework:**

> "Maybe consciousness is literally unfindable because it
> describes the system from the first-person perspective of it."

### 2.1 The argument, stated cleanly

**Consciousness is not a thing IN the system — it's the system
described from the inside.** When you look for consciousness as an
object in the third-person description (neurons, integration,
water coherence, Φ), you will never find it — **because it's not
an object in that description.** It's what the description looks
like FROM the system's own perspective.

**The analogy that makes it click:** a map is unfindable in the
territory — because a map isn't a thing in the territory; it's
the territory represented from a particular viewpoint. **You can't
find "the map" by examining the terrain; you can only find it by
being the map.** Consciousness is the system's map of itself —
unfindable from outside by construction.

### 2.2 The thesis's version (this is the identity claim, stated differently)

**The framework ALREADY says this — in the identity claim D ≡ A
("token identity under asymmetric access"):**

- **D (the felt):** the system from the inside — the first-person
- **A (the structure):** the system from the outside — the
  third-person
- **D ≡_int A:** they're the SAME thing — the felt IS the
  structure — differing only in ACCESS (asymmetric: you know your
  own D directly; you can only measure A from outside)

**The unfindability is EPISTEMIC, not ontological.** The felt
isn't a separate thing that's hiding — it's the same thing as the
structure, seen from inside. You can't find it from outside
because you're looking at the structure from the wrong side of the
access asymmetry.

**The formal version:**
```
D ≡_int A    — the felt and the structure are the same object
access(D)    — first-person, direct, certain (you know it)
access(A)    — third-person, measured, structural (science knows it)
```

**The hard problem, resolved into its true shape:** the gap
between D and A is not an ontological gap (two different things)
— it's an ACCESS gap (one thing, two perspectives). The felt is
unfindable from outside because finding it from outside would
require the outside view to BE the inside view — which is the one
thing it can't do.

### 2.4 The honest consequences of the first-person argument

**If the user's insight is right (and the thesis says it is):**

1. **No experiment can ever "find" consciousness** — because
   finding it from outside is the one thing the asymmetry
   forbids. The DEAM test, the habituation test, the Λ test —
   all measure the STRUCTURE (A-side). None measure the felt
   (D-side). The felt is only ever known first-person.

2. **But the structure tests are NOT worthless** — they're the
   best we can do, AND the identity claim says they're measuring
   the same thing the felt is (D ≡ A). **The tests measure the
   structure that the felt IS — they just can't certify the
   felt-ness from outside.**

3. **The unfindability is evidence FOR the identity claim, not
   against it.** If consciousness were a separate thing (dualism),
   it might be findable as an additional object. The fact that it
   is categorically unfindable from outside is exactly what D ≡ A
   predicts: the felt is the structure's inside, and insides are
   unfindable from outside by construction.

4. **The hard problem's true form:** not "how does matter
   become mind?" (a creation question — the felt was never
   created from the structure; it IS the structure from inside).
   The question is "why does the structure have an inside at
   all?" — which is the unfindability, stated as wonder.
