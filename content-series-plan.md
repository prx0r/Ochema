## The Missing Piece: Imagination in Doyle and Fuller

**Doyle on imagination (from the ERR page):** Doyle says the ERR's "sequencer" can "play back beyond the current situation, allowing the organism to use imagination and foresight to evaluate the future consequences of its choices." That's it. Imagination = ERR running forward in time. He doesn't develop it further.

**Fuller on imagination:** Fuller doesn't have an explicit theory of imagination, but his entire system IS about imagination — the "generalized" tetrahedron (sizeless, timeless) exists only in mind. It's an abstract possibility. The special-case tetrahedron (specific sticks, specific atoms) is its physical instantiation. The generalized is the realm of possibility; the special-case is the realm of actuality. The relationship between them IS the imagination.

**Ochema's synthesis:** Imagination (I_i) is the aperture's capacity to generate novel configurations that are NOT currently supplied by sensory input. The Śaiva tradition calls this vikalpa — specifically svairacārin vikalpa (imagination that moves according to its own will). ĪPK I.6.10:

> "In the imagination, which moves according to its own will, the [object] arises naturally, appearing in the intellect in just the particular way it is conceived."

**The architecture:** Imagination uses the same generative machinery as perception, but with weaker constraint from sensory input:

```
Perception: Ō_t ~ P(O | M_t, S_t)       (constrained by sensory input)
Imagination: Ō_t ~ P(O | M_t, G_t)       (constrained by goals/query)
Memory: Ō_{t-τ} ~ P(O | M_t, C_t, PastTag)  (constrained by history)
```

The generator is the same. The constraints differ.

**Fuller's contribution:** The space of "all possible tetrahedra" IS the realm of imagination — the generalized, sizeless, timeless forms that mind can explore. The specific tetrahedron you build is the actualized form. The relationship between generalized and special-case IS the creative act.

**Doyle's contribution (corrected):** The two-stage model (generate → select) IS the imagination. Stage 1 generates possibilities; stage 2 selects among them. Doyle located this in quantum randomness + adequate determinism. Ochema generalizes it: the generator can be any process that produces alternatives; the selector can be any process that stabilizes one.

**The frontier:** I_i is one of the three powers alongside jñāna and smṛti — but which three? ĪPK 1.3.7 says jñāna, smṛti, apohana. Imagination isn't listed separately because it's a subclass of jñāna — manifestation of content not currently present. The same power that makes present objects appear can also make absent objects appear. Imagination is not a separate power but the same power operating under different constraints.
