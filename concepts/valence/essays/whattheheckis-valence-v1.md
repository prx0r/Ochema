## The one-sentence version

**Valence is the coherence of the state: felt quality is a functional
of the geometry of possibility. Most of state space is a neutral hum —
and bliss and agony are the rare, real peaks of a heavy-tailed
distribution, not the average of experience.**

---

## The problem

The hard problem has a harder part. It is one thing to ask why there
is experience at all; it is another to ask why experience is *toned* —
why every moment is not just information but also feel: pleasant,
unpleasant, or the background hum of neither. A perception is never
raw; it arrives already liked or disliked, already colored.

And there is an empirical asymmetry that any theory of quality must
explain: **most of life is neither ecstasy nor agony.** The extremes
exist — the bliss of union, the agony of fragmentation — but they are
rare, intense, and short. Ordinary experience clusters near neutral.
Why should the distribution of felt quality look like that? A
functionalist might expect quality to be spread evenly across
experience. It is not.

The framework's answer: valence is not a layer added to perception.
It is a functional of the state's own geometry — and the shape of the
distribution is not an accident, it is the shape of state space
itself.

## The theorem (the core, driving)

**Valence ν_i is a coherence functional on the state space of the
bearer, with a heavy-tailed distribution (HTVH):**

```
ν_i = coherence functional(ω)      — felt quality IS the geometry
P(ν) ~ heavy-tailed                — most states near-neutral,
                                     extremes rare and intense
```

**The two anchors, from the core:**

1. **HTVH (Gómez-Emilsson & Percy 2023, peer-reviewed).** The
   heavy-tailed valence hypothesis: the distribution of felt valence
   across possible states is heavy-tailed — most possible experiences
   are mildly pleasant or neutral; extreme valence occupies a small
   but real region of state space. This is the first peer-reviewed
   anchor for a valence claim. **Grade: B.**
2. **The operator form (ochemamath §7).** Valence = Perelman entropy
   / a coherence functional on state space; quality as the spectrum
   of the modular Hamiltonian K = −log Δ. The "felt quality IS the
   geometry" claim now has a specific object. **Grade: A (math) / D
   (application).**

**The convergence (why this matters):** valence = coherence unifies the
whole range of experience in one variable — the bliss of unity is high
coherence, the suffering of fragmentation is low coherence, the
neutral hum of ordinary states is the dense mid-range of the heavy
tail. And the harmonic ladder (the octave structure) is valence's
skeleton: the coherent states are the resonant states — **the octaves
ARE the valence peaks.**

**Grade: B (the HTVH identification — peer-reviewed) / D (the
specific operator form — speculative).**

## What this means — three inputs

### The comparative sweep (traditions that stated it first)

- **The five blisses (ānanda) of Kashmir Śaivism:** experience
  culminates in bliss — not as a reward but as the *nature* of the
  coherent extreme. The tradition's claim: at full coherence, the tone
  of experience is bliss.
- **Buddhist vedanā:** the affective tone of every moment — valence is
  not a layer on perception; perception is feeling-shaped. The three
  tones (pleasant / unpleasant / neutral) are the tradition's coarse
  quantization of the same variable.
- **Rasa theory:** the aesthetic-emotional essence IS the experience —
  the taste of a moment is not what the moment means but what it *is*.
- **The corpus (Cannon, Seth):** "vibration compatibility" and "point
  of power" — the experiential tradition's statement that the state's
  coherence at threshold determines the outcome: valence as the weight
  at the moment of selection (the orchestration law's felt face).

### The modern literature (what the field actually says)

- **HTVH (Gómez-Emilsson & Percy 2023, B):** the heavy-tailed
  distribution as a peer-reviewed, testable claim about the shape of
  quality-space.
- **The QRI valence program (confrontation on record):** ALIGNS — the
  valence anchor is shared; the confrontation record
  (qri-valence-frontier.md) holds the details.
- **Hedonic neuroscience (B):** valence as a real, measurable
  dimension of experience — the empirical backbone that the coherence
  functional must reproduce.
- **Perelman entropy (A math / D app):** the candidate operator form —
  the geometry whose functional valence might be. Mathematics real;
  identification speculative.
- **Bennett 2025 (preprint, C):** "interpretation and value judgement
  are one and the same" — the orchestration law from the evolutionary
  side: valence IS the judgment, not a signal that feeds one. The
  framework's valence claim and Bennett's value-judgment claim are the
  same claim from two directions.

### The other views (rival theories)

- **Appraisal theories:** valence = evaluation, a step after
  perception. The framework's reply: if interpretation and judgment
  are one (Bennett), there is no step — the evaluation IS the
  state's coherence, not a separate computation on it.
- **Active inference (Friston):** valence as expected free energy — a
  mechanism candidate for the coherence functional, not a rival; the
  precision-weighting machinery (L4) is how the functional could be
  implemented.
- **IIT 4.0:** has no valence concept — the gap QRI has documented.
  The thesis's valence fills the hole IIT's own axiomatics leave open.
- **Panpsychism:** valence everywhere, in everything. The framework's
  narrower claim: valence is a functional of a state — where there are
  states with the modular structure, there is valence. The
  distribution is heavy-tailed because state space is.

## The honest caveat (rendered from the core)

The HTVH anchor is B — peer-reviewed and testable. The operator form
is D — the mapping from geometry to feeling is the claim, not the
evidence. **The spectral-quality test is unrun** (GAP): if quality is
the spectrum of the modular Hamiltonian K = −log Δ, then qualities are
the "frequencies" of the state's own time — and no experiment yet
discriminates the spectral hypothesis from mere correlation. The heavy
tail is the map; the spectrum is the untested frontier. Valence is the
one concept where the framework can point to a peer-reviewed anchor —
and it is still an anchor, not a proof.
