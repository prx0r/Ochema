## Phase 0 — The material state (what we had)

Before writing anything: the full session state was loaded — the
thesis (8 axioms, 8 layers, 6 invariants), the registries (17
confrontations, 10 GAPS, 13 concepts), the evidence tree, the LOG
(v1.15.1), the game corpus, and the peer review (v1.15.1 correction,
on record). **The concept is built from the existing graded material —
never from enthusiasm.**

## Phase 1 — The formal core question

**The discipline: is there a formal core?** (concept-object skill rule
#1: a concept needs a theorem-grade or graded claim, not just a
topic.) Consciousness is the meta-concept — its core is the identity
claim D_i ≡_int A_i plus the bearer (M, ω). Both exist in the thesis
with grades. So yes: formal core exists.

**The one-sentence formal statement** was extracted verbatim from the
thesis's language ("the intrinsic presence of a materially enacted
process that regulates its own future..."), not invented.

## Phase 2 — The rigour map (the key move)

The user's instruction: **grounding and math come from our framework,
and the inherited status or rigour comes from the frameworks we are
supported by / reject.** This became the core's structure:

1. **Supported by** — list every source that actually carries the
   concept, with its true grade: theorems (A), mainstream theory
   (IIT, B/C), formal twin (Kleiner, A), skeptics' concessions (B/C),
   measured biology (Levin, B), preprint (Bennett, C). **The grades
   are inherited from the sources' real status — not assigned by us.**
   This is the difference between "the framework says" and "the
   framework is supported by."

2. **Rejected** — every claim the framework rejects, at full strength,
   including the framework's OWN recent error (the nonlocal-encoding
   → reincarnation graft, v1.15.1). **The framework is a source of
   rejections too — and it rejects its own overreach first.**

## Phase 3 — The failure absorbed (the peer review)

The process note for this session must record the failure, because
it's the most instructive step:

- **What happened:** a conversational answer grafted real physics
  (HaPPY codes, Penington) onto reincarnation as if the math implied
  personal identity survives death.
- **The review's points (accepted in full):** the math concerns
  logical-qubit survival in a toy model — not persons. No mechanism
  connects entanglement structure to identity survival. The planarian
  result is real but is a body-shape pattern in LIVING tissue; the
  gradients stop at death. The honest position on reincarnation-as-
  transfer: no mechanism, no controlled evidence, mundane
  explanations exist. The grading scaffolding is house discipline,
  not peer review.
- **The fix (on record):** UPLOAD-SPEC.md relabeled as fiction;
  TREE.md GAP #10 updated; LOG v1.15.1 quotes the review at full
  strength.

**The rule extracted (now permanent): a metaphor is not a derivation.
Physics vocabulary grafted onto unfalsifiable claims is a category
error even when graded. The grading system is internal hygiene, not
peer review — and must never be presented as institutional
validation.**

## Phase 4 — Writing the core (the checklist)

For each section of core.md, the questions asked:

| Section | Question | Answer given |
|---|---|---|
| Formal statement | Is this the thesis's exact claim? | Yes — D_i ≡_int A_i, verbatim from the framework |
| Grade | What is the honest grade? | E (postulate), mainstreamed via IIT (B/C), architecture demonstrated (B/C) — all three, never just the flattering one |
| Supported by | What ACTUALLY carries this? | Theorems, mainstream theory, formal twin, concessions, measured biology — each with its true status |
| Rejected | What does the framework reject? | Everything — including its own overreach |
| Evidence | What's in TREE.md? | Only the graded rows, supports AND negates |
| Gaps | What's exposed? | The 6 open GAPS, including the survival claims held as unsupported |
| Connected | What links to it? | The 8 related concept cores |

**The key discipline: every claim in the core traces to a graded
source in the framework — nothing is new, nothing is improved,
nothing is softened.**

## Phase 5 — The essay rendering

The essay is a RENDERING of the core, not a new argument:
- One sentence first (the accessible form of the formal statement)
- The honest standing first (the grades, before the excitement)
- The architecture (the six invariants — from the core)
- The supported/rejected map (from the core)
- The caveat rendered from the core's gaps
- Header records WHICH core version it renders (sync discipline)

**If the core says C, the essay says "candidate, not established" —
never "we know."** (concept-object skill, pitfall #2.)

## Phase 6 — Registration + log

- concept.json written to schema (formal_core, essays, evidence,
  renderings)
- REGISTRY.md updated (row added, status active)
- LOG.md entry + version bump (minor: new concept)
- **Audit rule:** the essay's rendered_from must match the core's
  version; the JSON's renderings.whattheheckis points at the current
  essay

## The skill (what to automate from this)

When creating ANY concept, the process is:
1. Load the graded material (thesis + TREE.md + confrontations) —
   never write from memory of the conversation
2. Extract the formal statement VERBATIM from the thesis
3. Build the rigour map: supported-by (true grades, inherited) and
   rejected (at full strength, including self-rejections)
4. Check for category errors: is any metaphor being used as a
   derivation? (The v1.15.1 rule.)
5. Write the core with the honesty checklist (section by section)
6. Render the essay as a current-state rendering, caveat included
7. Register, log, bump — and audit the sync

**And the standing reminder, at every step: the framework is a
convergence study with internal grades — not an established research
program. The grades are house hygiene. The identity is postulated.
The survival claims are unsupported. The burden is unmet. That is
the foundation — and it's strong enough to build on precisely
because it says so.**
