## The one-sentence version

**Time is not a stage. It is the flow a state generates out of itself — and when the state changes, time itself changes.**

---

## The problem

We experience time as a river. Physics says it's a block. Neither
feels right, because both assume time is *something* — a background,
a dimension, a flow that exists whether or not anything is in it.

What if time isn't a thing at all? What if it's a *property of a
state* — like temperature is a property of a gas?

## The theorem

In 1970, Tomita and Takesaki proved something that looks like pure
math and turns out to be about time: **any state of a quantum system
generates its own flow.** Given a state ω (the "register" — the
configuration of a process), there is a unique one-parameter family
of transformations σ_t^ω, and the state is "thermal" with respect to
it — it doesn't change under its own flow.

In 1994, Connes and Rovelli asked: what if THIS is what time is? In a
theory where there's no external clock (general relativity), the
physical time would be the flow the state itself generates. They
checked: the time flow generated by the cosmic microwave background's
thermal state is exactly the Friedmann–Robertson–Walker time of the
expanding universe. **The universe's time comes from the universe's
state.**

## What this means

1. **Time is derived, not given.** The state determines the time.
   No state, no time. This is Spanda's claim from the 9th century —
   "the non-sequential action is the source of time and space" — as a
   theorem.

2. **The moment has three phases.** Any change in a state has the
   same structure: emergence (the new state forms), persistence (the
   state's own flow carries it), withdrawal (the state transitions to
   the next). Sṛṣṭi–sthiti–saṃhāra. Emission–persistence–withdrawal.
   The moment-triad. When the state changes, **time itself is
   redefined** — the new state has its own flow.

3. **The present is the only point of power.** Within a state's flow,
   everything is determined — the past and future of that flow are
   fixed. But the transition — the moment — is where the state can
   change. Seth's "the point of power is in the present" is
   physically exact. Ibn Arabi's "the indivisible instant" — each
   instant is a new creation — is the same structure as theology.

4. **Your time is your own.** The state of your register determines
   your time-structure. The orchestration law: the state at threshold
   determines the outcome — and the state determines the time in
   which outcomes occur.

## The levels of time

| Level | What it is |
|---|---|
| The flow's structure | The invariant — what doesn't change when the state changes |
| The instant | A single transition — the flash, the moment |
| The sequence | Emergent time — the flow's trajectory, the river we experience |

These aren't three kinds of time. They're three views of one
structure: the invariant, the transition, the trajectory.

## The honest caveat

The theorem is proved. Whether physical time IS the modular flow is
the Connes–Rovelli hypothesis — well-supported, not established.
The experiment that would decide it is one of the thesis's open
frontiers: find a system whose observed time demonstrably differs
from any state's modular flow, or confirm it everywhere you look.

Until then: time is the best candidate we have for what the
traditions always said — **the pulse of the state, generating its
own river.**
