# What the Heck Is Spanda?

---

Good, let's do this without symbols — just the ideas, what the text actually says, and why the doctrine matters for a physics-grade picture of reality.

## The one-sentence version

Reality is not a thing and not a collection of things. It is a pulse — a self-sustaining vibration of awareness that creates, maintains, and withdraws everything, including space and time, which are *derived* from it, not prior to it.

---

## The core claim: reality is an activity, not a substance

The Spanda doctrine (9th-century Kashmir, root text the Spandakārikā, "Stanzas on Vibration," attributed to Vasugupta, systematized by Kallata and Kṣemarāja) makes one radical move: it takes the dynamic character of consciousness — its *activity* — as primary. Not "there is a consciousness that happens to move," but: the movement IS what consciousness is.

Mark Dyczkowski's *The Doctrine of Vibration* (the definitive study):

> "Spanda, the vibration or pulse of consciousness. The mainstay of the Doctrine of Vibration is the contemplative experience the awakened yogi has of his true nature as the universal perceiving and acting consciousness. Every activity in the universe, as well as every perception, notion, sensation or emotion in the microcosm ebbs and flows as part of the universal rhythm of the one reality."

Key term: **sphurattā** — literally "radiant pulse" or "glittering." The light of consciousness is not a static glow; it is a shimmering, a throbbing. The pulse IS the light.

The relationship between the power and its possessor is itself the pulse:

> "The pulse of their union and separation is the archetype of the fusion and divergence of opposites, through the interplay of the elements constituting the cosmic order that takes place in harmony with their recurrent emergence and subsidence into the absolute."

## The machinery: one pulse, three moments

The Spandakārikā's very first stanza gives the whole program:

> "We praise that Śaṅkara who is the source of the power of the Wheel of Energies by whose expansion (unmeṣa) and contraction (nimeṣa) the universe is absorbed and comes into being."

Two phases — expansion and contraction — but they are not two things. They are the same will, seen in its two directions. The doctrine is explicit that Śiva never changes; the "expansion" and "contraction" are figurative descriptions of one eternal activity:

> "The terms 'expansion' and 'contraction' as well as the activity they figuratively denote, refer exclusively to the one thing which is in fact inherently related to Śaṅkara, namely, His will (icchāmātra) which is His eternal attribute and essential nature."

Any event — cosmic or mental — has three moments: **emission** (srṣṭi, coming into being), **persistence** (sthiti, abiding), **withdrawal** (laya, subsiding). The doctrine maps these onto the three powers of will, knowledge, and action:

> "All things come into being through an act of will, with action as its immediate instrumental cause and knowledge of its application as the intermediary... this three-fold vibration constitutes the essential nature of all things as elements of experience."

This is the minimal structure of any happening whatsoever: arise, abide, dissolve. The doctrine's claim is that this three-moment structure is not a description of change — it IS the nature of reality, the pulse.

## The Wheel of Energies (śakticakra)

The universe is a wheel of powers. Stanza 1 calls Śiva "the source of the power of the Wheel of Energies." The wheel has concentric circles:

- **Khecari** (moving in space) — the universal powers: omnipotence, omniscience, perfect completeness, freedom from natural law, eternality
- **Gocari** (moving in the senses) — the mental powers: determination, self-identification, thought-construction
- **Dikcari** (moving in the directions) — the ten sensory powers

And the key move: the SAME five universal powers, when contracted, become the five **kañcukas** — the coverings that constitute finite individuality:

| Universal power | Contracted (kañcuka) |
|---|---|
| Omnipotence | Kalā — limited agency |
| Omniscience | Vidyā — limited knowledge |
| Completeness | Rāga — attachment, limited desire |
| Freedom from law | Niyati — necessity, limited causality |
| Eternality | Kāla — time, limitation |

The wheel's full cycle (in Krama doctrine: the Twelve Kālīs) is the closed loop of powers — creation, maintenance, destruction, and the states between. The fettered soul is caught in the wheel; the awakened recognizes the wheel as his own activity.

## The claim about space and time (the part that matters for physics)

This is the deepest passage in the Doctrine of Vibration. Action is of two kinds:

> "The first is the kind of activity that can be broken down into a series (krama) or sequence of actions set in time and space. The second type is the non-successive (akramika) action that takes place within the absolute. **It is the source of time and space** and hence beyond the spatial and temporal distinctions which characterize all sequences."

And the three levels of temporality:

> "Reality contemplated from the highest (para) level of consciousness is experienced as a single, unchanging whole. At the lower (apara) level we experience this same reality as a sequence of events — as changing positions in space and a continuous transition from one moment in time to the next. At the intermediate (parapara) level, reality is experienced at the instant of cosmic manifestation into which it blossoms with the suddenness and energy of a lightning flash. This cosmic expansion (visvasphāra) occurs in a single, non-sequential flash."

Read soberly: **space and time are not the stage. They are derived — the sequential appearance of a non-sequential activity.** The pulse is prior to space and time; space and time are what the pulse looks like when viewed in sequence.

## Where the vibration is experienced

Stanza 22 is the famous practical instruction:

> "Spanda is stable in the state one enters when extremely angry, intensely excited, running or wondering what to do."

The point is not "get angry to find God." The point is precise: in the *moment before* the emotion takes hold — the bare instant of excitation — the universal pulse is visible, because the mind has not yet constructed its reaction. The doctrine distinguishes:

- **Universal (stable) Spanda** — the eternal activity, "free of alterations," the main thing to be realized
- **Particular (transient) Spanda** — the individual pulsations of pleasure, pain, perception

And Stanza 3 states the invariant:

> "Even when division prevails due to the waking and other states, it extends through that which is undivided, because the perceiving subjectivity forever remains true to its own nature."

Across waking, dreaming, deep sleep — the perceiver does not change. The pulse has a stable core: the subject-index, the invariant.

## Why it matters

If the convergence holds, the Spanda doctrine is the earliest known statement of the deepest structural result in modern quantum gravity: **the stage is derived from the actor.** Not "consciousness exists in space and time" but "space and time are the sequential appearance of a non-sequential activity." The pulse is primary; the sequence is emergent; the invariant survives.

Spanda says the same about reality as a whole: not a thing, not a collection — a pulse whose expansion and contraction ARE the universe coming into being and being absorbed.
