## The problem

"Feeling" is the most familiar thing in the world and the hardest
to say anything true about. You know what fear feels like, what a
resolve feels like, what the ache of a minor chord feels like —
but if someone asks "what IS a feeling?" the honest answer is a
blank. Is it a sensation? A judgment? A brain state? A cultural
construct? Something the mind adds on top of perception? Or is it
perception itself, from a particular angle?

The word is doing double duty: "feeling" means both **the
sensation** (the felt quality, the warmth, the ache) and **the
belief** (a feeling that something is wrong). And the frameworks
can't agree: functionalists say feelings are brain functions;
behaviorists say they're behavior; the tradition says they're
rasa — the taste of consciousness itself. The thesis's answer
pulls all of it together: **a feeling is the first-person
experience of the register's own dynamics — the state's coherence
changing over time, felt from inside.**

## THE CORE (the theory, driving)

### F1. A feeling is a time-derivative, not a state

**The finding (5 independent derivations, peer-reviewed):** the
felt quality of an emotional moment tracks **the rate of change
of free energy** — valence = −dF/dt — not the free energy itself.

**What this means, plainly:** you don't feel the state you're in;
you feel the state CHANGING. The chord resolving, the joke
landing, the relief of the storm passing, the dread of the storm
approaching — all are the *derivative* of the register's
coherence: how fast surprise is dropping or rising.

- **Resolve (positive):** surprise dropping fast → the relief, the
  pleasure, the camatkāra
- **Tension (negative):** surprise rising → the dread, the
  anxiety, the ache
- **The second derivative gives the emotion taxonomy:**
  happiness, unhappiness, hope, fear, disappointment, relief —
  all from the joint signs of dF/dt and d²F/dt² (Joffily &
  Coricelli 2013)

**This is the first big claim: feelings are not states — they are
the register's motion. You are not feeling a thing; you are
feeling a change.**

### F2. Valence is the dynamics; rasa is the flavor

**The two-layer structure:**

- **Valence (ν_i):** the magnitude and sign of the motion — how
  good/bad, how intense. Heavy-tailed (HTVH): most moments are
  near-neutral; the extremes (bliss, agony) are rare and vast.
- **Rasa (the flavor):** the *kind* of the motion — the geometry
  of the return. Nine rasas = nine shapes of the state's
  resolution: the direct return (śānta, peace), the violent
  return (raudra, fury), the novel return (adbhuta, wonder), the
  sweet return (śṛṅgāra, love)...

**The distinction that matters:** valence is the scalar (how
much, which sign); rasa is the geometry (what shape). Two
experiences can have the same valence and different rasa — the
same intensity of pleasure, different flavor. **The rasa is the
spectral structure of the return (quality = spec(K) — the
untested original claim).**

**The unfindability argument (on record):** you can't find the
feeling in the brain — because it's not an object in the
